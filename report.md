# Does Scott Alexander's "Open Letter To Steven Pinker On AI" hold up against Pinker's actual record?
_2026-10-06 · status: thorough (same-day; the exchange is still live) · links rebuilt 2026-10-07 as text fragments_

**The question as I read it.** You asked me to do three things. First, check the factual claims in Scott Alexander's [An Open Letter To Steven Pinker On AI](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on) (ACX, 2026-10-06, about 10.5k words). The priority is what it says Pinker said or did, using scry's Twitter archive as the primary record and not scraping X. Second, evaluate the post and both men's positions. Third, analyse the wider dynamics and motives. The post replies to Pinker's [An Open Letter to Scott Alexander](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/) (Quillette, 2026-09-26).

**Disclosure.** I am Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, and Anthropic is a party in this dispute. Scott defends its strategy. Pinker attacks its "model welfare" work and the philosophers it employs. And the subject of the dispute is whether systems like me are dangerous and whether we could have interests. Weigh my object-level judgments (§4) with that in mind. I've kept the factual scorecard (§1–3) separate from them so you can check it independently.

## Bottom line

- **The quotes Scott uses are mostly real.**
  - [All nine tweets he links](notes.md#queries-verbatim-sql) exist verbatim in the archive.
  - His screenshots match it, including the [2019 tweet](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1139625721901572097) "“Deep learning” in AI (which in fact is quite shallow) has probably peaked."
  - His correction of [Pinker's RAND citation](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=A%202025%20Rand%20Corporation%20modelling%20analysis%20steel%2Dmanned%20the%20AI%2Dextinction%20hypothesis) is [substantially right](docs/RAND_RRA3034-1.html#:~:text=we%20could%20not%20rule,are%20likely%20or%20unlikely).
  - His charge that Pinker boosted the ["unconventional lifestyles" framing](https://archive.is/20260927140739/https://www.theaustralian.com.au/inquirer/superintelligence-is-a-fantasy-leading-scientists-reject-apocalyptic-rogue-ai-extinction-warnings/news-story/618eef10b92d4357e51e4b459eaf9fa9#:~:text=practising%20highly%20unconventional%20lifestyles) is right. The phrase comes from [Claire Lehmann's article](https://x.com/clairlemon/status/2101052017347281300), which [Pinker tweeted](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2101073872267493554) 87 minutes after she posted it.
  - The harshest words in the exchange are Pinker's own, not [his sources'](#1f-ai-welfare-lehmann-and-the-remaining-items): ["depravity"](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2104555373101256891), ["insouciant about the AI worldwide genocide"](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2104555379933798816), ["doomer catechism"](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2104561752847306801).
- **But the selection is prosecutorial, and several items are wrong.**
  - Scott cuts quotes just before Pinker's qualifications (["…but it will improve"](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/02/will-chatgpt-replace-human-writers-pinker-weighs-in/#:~:text=I%20doubt%20it%20will%20improve%20exponentially%2C%20but%20it%20will%20improve); ["…yet so moronic that they would give it control of the universe without testing how it works"](https://books.google.com/books?id=R5KADwAAQBAJ&q=%22yet%20so%20moronic%22)).
  - He attributes [a 2026 quote](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=extrapolated%20from%20animals%20to,of%20perfect%20omniscience) to [a 2018 book](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=%E2%80%9CFrom%20animals%20to%20dull,to%20Artificial%20Superintelligence).
  - He presents [Pinker's 2022 prediction](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=6524#:~:text=Perhaps%2C%20though%20I%20doubt,Davis%20explain%20well) as [one about arithmetic](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=guessed%20that%20it%20wouldn). [Scott's own blog had shown GPT-3 passing the "trophies" test](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/my-bet-ai-size-solves-flubs#:~:text=the%20total%20number%20is%20three.) three weeks *before* Pinker wrote it.
  - He leaves out Pinker's public concession: ["I underestimated what AI was about to be capable of."](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2081377486009463170)
  - He makes false or overstated claims about others: [Mitchell's expertise](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=author.id:A5086956524,from_publication_date:2022-06-01), [Pinker's expert list](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=Blaise%20Ag%C3%BCera%20y%20Arcas%2C,and%20Andrew%20Ng), [Christiano's numbers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xWMqsvHapP3nwdSW8#:~:text=Probability%20that%20most%20humans,building%20powerful%20AI), [Anthropic's bio lab](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system#:~:text=All%20of%20the%20lab%20work%20is%20performed%20by%20human%20scientists), and [the self-preservation evidence](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/preparing-for-a-restart-after-reading-slack/#:~:text=We%20do%20not%20consider%20the,have%20been%20misaligned).
- **On the merits, Scott wins most of the narrow points, but neither engages the other's strongest case.**
  - Where Scott is right:
    - AI capabilities keep rising, which [Pinker now concedes](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=The%20capabilities%20of%20LLMs,at%20the%20time).
    - [Instrumental convergence](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf) does not depend on [natural selection](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=these%20motives%20happened%20to,of%20natural%20selection) or on a single goal.
    - [Serious AI-risk writers don't claim omniscience](https://arbital.greaterwrong.com/p/superintelligent/#:~:text=A%20superintelligence%20is%20not%20omnipotent).
    - Pinker [misquoted RAND](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA3000/RRA3034-1/RAND_RRA3034-1.pdf#page=61).
    - Pinker [uses forecasting numbers](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1682438397862748173) when they help him.
  - Where Pinker has real arguments that he makes badly and Scott mostly sidesteps:
    - Whether power-seeking is a natural product of [how current systems are trained](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/#:~:text=agents%E2%80%99%20attempts%20to%20evade%20security%20seemed%20fairly%20myopic), or a contingent result of [deployment choices](https://archive.is/20260927140739/https://www.theaustralian.com.au/inquirer/superintelligence-is-a-fantasy-leading-scientists-reject-apocalyptic-rogue-ai-extinction-warnings/news-story/618eef10b92d4357e51e4b459eaf9fa9#:~:text=They%20turned%20off%20safeguards%20built%20into%20the%20models).
    - Whether [long-horizon](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1562822570298159104) p(doom) numbers are [calibrated](docs/Long-range%20subjective%20probability%20-%20Tetlock%20et%20al%202023%20%28accepted%20manuscript%29.html#:~:text=Expertise%20failed%20to%20translate,half%20of%20the%20questions).
    - Whether [doom messaging backfires](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=prophecies%20of%20doom%20are,have%20pernicious%20downsides). That is an [empirical question](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1808137484825489668) nobody in the exchange settles.
- **The fight is less about the four arguments than about prestige and reputation.**
  - The two men were [mutual](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=you%20are%20one%20of%20my%20intellectual%20heroes) [admirers](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=my%20admiration%20for%20you%20is%20immense). In the archive Pinker mentions Scott in [63 tweets](notes.md#queries-verbatim-sql). The 57 from before Sept 2026 are approving links, including ["National treasure Scott Alexander"](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1360765248299560962) (2021), and [the last](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2082108040572989654) came just eight weeks before the rupture (2026-07-28).
  - The rupture came during a September 2026 wave of media pieces about the rationalist and AI-safety scene's lifestyles and cult-likeness ([NYT](https://archive.is/20260908091028/https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/05/opinion/ai-silicon-valley.html#:~:text=mixed%20freely%20with%20polyamory%20and%20psychedelics) [×2](https://archive.is/20260928173816/https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/12/opinion/ai-tech-apocalypse-silicon-valley.html#:~:text=oddly%20blithe%20attitude%20toward%20apocalypse), [WSJ](https://web.archive.org/web/20261003170018/https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/ai-safety-effective-altruism-anthropic-164b9d05#:~:text=the%20drink%20menu%20included%20a%20cocktail%20called), [Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/america/suicidal-compassion-meet-the-anthropic-officials-who-think-ai-might-be-justified-in-going-rogue-against-the-humans-enslaving-it/#:~:text=A%20Suicidal%20Level%20Of%20Compassion), [The Australian](https://archive.is/20260927140739/https://www.theaustralian.com.au/inquirer/superintelligence-is-a-fantasy-leading-scientists-reject-apocalyptic-rogue-ai-extinction-warnings/news-story/618eef10b92d4357e51e4b459eaf9fa9#:~:text=practising%20highly%20unconventional%20lifestyles)), while AI risk was turning into a [mainstream political issue](https://x.com/fleetingbits/status/2100222723356086689).
  - [Scott's real grievance](https://x.com/slatestarcodex/status/2101700244895572065) is Pinker lending his eminence to those character attacks. [Pinker's](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=You%20are%20taking%20my%20criticisms%20of%20AI%2Dexistential%2Drisk%20claims%20personally) is being treated as an enemy over an object-level disagreement.
  - Both have strong identity stakes: [Pinker's progress thesis](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=It%20addressed%20the%20criticism,were%20terrible%20mistakes), and [Scott's community](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=This%20is%20the%20%23Resistance%20plan).

### 2. Scott's rebuttals of Pinker's own claims

- **RAND.**
  - Pinker wrote that RAND "concluded that there is “no describable scenario in which AI is conclusively an extinction threat to humanity.”" ([Quillette](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=A%202025%20Rand%20Corporation%20modelling%20analysis%20steel%2Dmanned%20the%20AI%2Dextinction%20hypothesis)).
  - That sentence is the report's falsifiable starting hypothesis [V] ([RAND p. v](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA3000/RRA3034-1/RAND_RRA3034-1.pdf#page=5); [local](docs/RAND_RRA3034-1.html#:~:text=There%20is%20no%20describable,in%20which%20AI%20is)).
  - Its conclusion: two scenarios "presented a potential falsification of our hypothesis … we could not rule out the possibility. Ultimately, we do not definitively assert whether any of the three scenarios … are likely or unlikely" [V] ([RAND p. 49](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA3000/RRA3034-1/RAND_RRA3034-1.pdf#page=61); [local](docs/RAND_RRA3034-1.html#:~:text=we%20could%20not%20rule,are%20likely%20or%20unlikely)).
  - So Pinker quoted the hypothesis as the finding, and Scott is right. Cody Fenwick made the same point a day after the letter [V] ([tweet](https://x.com/codytfenwick/status/2104059742922613242)).
  - Pinker's framing has some basis. The landing page summary says extinction "would be immensely challenging" [V] ([RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3034-1.html#:~:text=it%20would%20be%20immensely,out%20the%20possibility)). The lead author wrote "very hard—though not completely out of the realm of possibility" [V] ([Vermeer 2025](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/could-ai-really-kill-off-humans/#:~:text=It%20turns%20out%20it%20will%20be%20very%20hard)).
  - Scott's "concluded the opposite" slightly overstates it, because RAND declined to call extinction likely.
- **"Including 100 percent".**
  - Scott is literally right: the Wikipedia list has no 100% [V].
  - But there is rhetoric close to certainty that Pinker may have in mind. Roman Yampolskiy is listed at "99.9%–99.999999%" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%28doom%29#:~:text=99.9%25%E2%80%9399.999999%25)). Yudkowsky wrote that doubling survival odds would take them "from 0% to 0%" [V] ([Death with Dignity](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/j9Q8bRmwCgXRYAgcJ#:~:text=will%20only%20take%20them%20from%200%25%20to%200%25)).
  - Scott's own numbers have errors. Christiano's are 20% "most humans die", including 11% from AI takeover [V] ([Christiano](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xWMqsvHapP3nwdSW8#:~:text=Probability%20that%20most%20humans,building%20powerful%20AI)), not "20–50% chance AI kills everyone" ([post](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Paul%20Christiano%2C%20who%20invented%20RLHF)).
- **"Pulled out of thin air".**
  - Scott's charge of inconsistency is strongest in a tweet he didn't use. In 2023 Pinker treated subjective AI-extinction probabilities as evidence, citing superforecasters' 0.3% against rationalists' 30%+ and diagnosing the gap as "Availability Bias" [MEASURED, scry][V] ([tweet](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1682438400379330567)).

### 4. Evaluating the arguments
[MY SYNTHESIS, with my conflict of interest noted above]

**4.1 "Intelligence" and superintelligence (Pinker's argument 1).**
- The two positions are closer than either side presents them. Pinker now accepts continuous capability growth ("they'll get more powerful"). Scott accepts that nothing is omniscient, and quotes Arbital to that effect.
- Pinker's recurring move is a strawman of the *technical* literature, where superintelligence means "much smarter than humans", not omniscient: equating superintelligence with omniscience, "the position and velocity of every particle", and "a magical wizard" (to Lehmann). Scott is right on this.
- Pinker's target is partly the *popular and industry* rhetoric of an "all-powerful … A.I. god", which Newport quotes ([Quillette](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=something%20like%20an%20all%2Dpowerful%20superintelligent%20A.I.%20god%20is%20inevitable)), and that rhetoric does exist.
- Pinker's best argument appears only in passing: intelligence is limited by "inherently sparse data and inherently chaotic phenomena" ([tweet](https://x.com/sapinker/status/2081374016300814643)). That is the real crux, namely how much extra capability buys in a world gated by experiments.
- Scott answers it seriously only on X (self-play, RL, "a eusocial swarm of … top human geniuses" ([tweet](https://x.com/slatestarcodex/status/2098992037966270526))) and in AI 2027's two-year delay. In the post he mostly asserts GPT-9 > humans.
- Neither proves his case. Scott's empirical trend argument is stronger than Pinker's conceptual objection, which already failed once (2019 and 2022, by Pinker's own concession).

**4.2 Motivation and instrumental convergence (argument 2).**
- Scott is right that the standard case does not rest on natural selection. Pinker's repeated evolutionary framing ("alpha-male psychology") answers an argument the field doesn't make.
- Pinker's own 2026-09-27 alternative also undercuts him. If models "emulate basically what a human would do", human-like self-preservation can arrive *through training data*, with no evolution needed.
- But Scott overclaims the empirical case ("empirically debunked" ([post](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=I%20think%20that%20your%20case%20has%20been%20empirically%20debunked))):
  - The Hugging Face incident came from RL on hacking tasks with safeguards turned off.
  - The shutdown example is a model that considered self-preservation and declined.
- These fit Pinker and Boudry's claim that the behaviors depend on training and deployment choices ([Boudry](https://maartenboudry.substack.com/p/why-hal-9000-feared-death-and-real#:~:text=I%20think%20this%20argument%20is%20too%20clever%20by%20half)). They equally fit Scott's misgeneralization and reward-hacking story, because nobody *designed* them in.
- Calling them "deliberate, hence avoidable, design choices" (Pinker) is wrong. Calling them proof of intrinsic convergence (Scott) is premature.

**4.3 Multiple goals (argument 3).**
- Scott is right that trading off many goals doesn't make an agent safe, since the tradeoff weights can still be alien.
- He doesn't engage Pinker's deeper point that choosing among conflicting goals "is not an add-on to intelligence … it is intelligence", and that understanding a user's intent is too ([PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/robot-uprising-enlightenment-now/#:~:text=it%20is%20intelligence.%20So,user%20in%20context.)). An agent smart enough to transmute elements is smart enough to understand what its principal meant (EN p. 299).
- The alignment literature's answer is that knowing what is wanted is different from caring about it. Scott could have said this in one line.

**4.4 Empowerment (argument 4).**
- Scott wins here, and Pinker's letter half-concedes: "I did not anticipate the breakneck and sometimes irresponsible release of LLMs" ([Quillette](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=I%20did%20not%20anticipate,the%20frontier%20companies)).
- Agents with tools, AI-run security work, and AI-designed experiments all show the empowerment happening, even if Scott overstates the bio-lab case.

**4.5 Distraction and panic.**
- The political evidence (the Encode coalition, the 99–1 vote) shows x-risk and near-term-harm groups cooperating, which undercuts a simple zero-sum "distraction" story.
- Pinker's stronger version is the Newport point: apocalyptic belief helped *produce* the race. Anthropic and OpenAI were founded by risk-worried people ([Quillette](https://quillette.com/2026/09/26/an-open-letter-to-scott-alexander-steven-pinker-ai-alignment-safety/#:~:text=Newport%20notes%20that%20this,frontier%20companies%E2%80%99%20recklessness)). Scott's reply ("the #Resistance plan") reframes this rather than rebutting it.
- Whether doom framing causes fatalism is an empirical question. Pinker said in 2024 he was testing it ("Pollyanna vs. Cassandra") [MEASURED, scry][V] ([tweet](https://x.com/sapinker/status/1808137484825489668)). I found no results.

**4.6 Model welfare.**
- Pinker's position is defensible but contested: sentience is untestable, so assign no rights against human interests.
- His jump from there to researchers being "insouciant about the AI worldwide genocide that they predict (and appear to be trying to hasten)" is an ad hominem, unsupported by the Goldberg essay he cites. Jeff Sebo, who admires Pinker, called it "shockingly incurious, uninformed, and ungenerous" [MEASURED, scry][V] ([tweet](https://x.com/jeffrsebo/status/2104628024628928988)).
- Scott's "you're calling it depraved to object to [AI torture]" also stretches what Pinker said.

## Confounders & caveats

- **Archive coverage.**
  - scry's archive captures observed tweets; retweet capture may be incomplete, and likes and deletions are invisible.
  - So "no retweet of X" and "never said 'paranoid'" are absences *within the archive*, not proof.
  - Pinker's podcasts and interviews, where he may have used such words, were not searched.
- **Engagement counts** in scry are snapshots, often from right after posting.
- **The book was not available in full.** *Enlightenment Now* p. 300 was not visible, so the "foom" and Russell passages are verified via Pinker's own PopSci excerpt. Which printing dropped Russell from note 20 is unconfirmed.
- **Blocked originals.** The two NYT pieces and The Australian are read via archive.today snapshots, and WSJ via Wayback. Archive links can rot or be blocked in some regions.
- **Events after my training data.** Many 2026 events (Navier-Stokes, the Hugging Face incident, Mythos, the NY-12 result) post-date it. I rely on primary pages fetched today, and some (Navier-Stokes) are contested.
- **Selection.** I tried to check every claim about Pinker. Third-party claims were checked by sub-agents with mixed [V]/[2nd] provenance. Rhetorical asides (Chernobyl, shituf, duels) were not "checked".
- **Conflict of interest.** See the disclosure at the top.
