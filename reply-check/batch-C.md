# Batch C: the doom literature (Section II and the omnipotence passages)

Pinker's essay: `../pinker-quillette-reply-2026-11-23.md`. Saved sources are in `sources/C-*`. Checked 2026-11-24.

Summary: every quotation is verbatim (one exception: C10, one word changed). What varies is what the quotation is
doing. Four of the "doom literature" items are fiction their authors label as a story, a worst case, or a
lower-bound illustration (C2, C5, C6, and Bostrom in C1). Two are cut at a conditional that Pinker's own paragraph
needs (C9 "repattern", C6 ICBMs). The biggest finding is C14: the phrase "hopefully lasting decades" is Scott's own
description of the plan he favours, and "resign en masse" is a proposal Scott's same essay argues against.

### C1. Bostrom, Superintelligence (2014): nanofactories
- **Pinker:** "the philosopher Nick Bostrom (famous for venturing that an AI given the goal of making paper clips would consume the world's resources, including our bodies) imagined that 'at a pre-set time, nanofactories producing nerve gas or target-seeking mosquito-like robots might then burgeon forth simultaneously from every square meter of the globe.'" (L42)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 verbatim, but it is one illustrative sentence from a longer scenario, and the sentence continues past where Pinker stops.
- **Evidence:**
  - "… pre-set time, nanofactories producing nerve gas or target-seeking mosquito-like robots might then burgeon forth simultaneously from every square meter of the globe (although more effective ways of killing could probably be devised by a machine with …" ([Google Books search snippet, Superintelligence, OUP 2014, id C-_8AwAAQBAJ](https://books.google.com/books?id=C-_8AwAAQBAJ&q=%22nerve+gas%22)) [2nd] (snippet text read via the Google Books feed API, not a page image; wording matches Pinker's)
  - Bostrom's own definition, which Pinker could have used for C10: "any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest" (Superintelligence, p. 22, as quoted in Google Books snippets) [2nd]
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - Bostrom's verb is "might", and he adds that a smarter machine would find "more effective ways of killing". That is Bostrom saying the example is a lower-grade illustration, not a forecast of nerve-gas mosquitos.
  - I could not retrieve the surrounding paragraph (Google Books returns only snippets; the book page is behind a captcha for scripts), so I cannot say which chapter's scenario this sits in. Pinker's gloss "imagined" is fair.
  - The paperclip parenthesis is the standard attribution (Bostrom 2003), not checked here.
- **How checked:** Google Books feed API (`www.google.com/books/feeds/volumes?q=...`) snippet; the web pages of the book are captcha-blocked from curl.

### C2. Yudkowsky, "AGI Ruin: A List of Lethalities"
- **Pinker:** "Yudkowsky … suggests that a superintelligence could email DNA sequences to companies that synthesise proteins, persuade an unwitting human to mix them in a beaker, and thereby build a nanofactory, after which: 'The nanomachinery builds diamondoid bacteria … strike on a timer.' The upshot is that 'Losing a conflict with a high-powered cognitive system looks at least as deadly as "everybody on the face of the Earth suddenly falls over dead within the same second."'" (L44–48)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 verbatim and a fair paraphrase of the scenario; but Yudkowsky presents it as a lower bound on what a smart system could do, which Pinker's "The upshot is" skips.
- **Evidence:**
  - "My lower-bound model of "how a sufficiently powerful intelligence would kill everyone, if it didn't want to not do that" is that it gets access to the Internet, emails some DNA sequences to any of the many many online firms that will take a DNA sequence in the email and ship you back proteins, and bribes/persuades some human who has no idea they're dealing with an AGI to mix proteins in a beaker, which then form a first-stage nanofactory which can build the actual nanomachinery." ([AGI Ruin, item 2](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uMQ3cqWDPHhjtiesc/agi-ruin-a-list-of-lethalities#:~:text=My%20lower%2Dbound%20model%20of,would%20kill%20everyone)) [V]
  - "The nanomachinery builds diamondoid bacteria, that replicate with solar power and atmospheric CHON, maybe aggregate into some miniature rockets or jets so they can ride the jetstream to spread across the Earth's atmosphere, get into human bloodstreams and hide, strike on a timer. Losing a conflict with a high-powered cognitive system looks at least as deadly as "everybody on the face of the Earth suddenly falls over dead within the same second"." ([same](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uMQ3cqWDPHhjtiesc/agi-ruin-a-list-of-lethalities#:~:text=Losing%20a%20conflict%20with,as%20deadly%20as)) [V]
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - The surrounding sentence in the original: "The concrete example I usually use here is nanotech, because there's been pretty detailed analysis of what definitely look like physically attainable lower bounds on what should be possible with nanotech, and those lower bounds are sufficient to carry the point." So the claim Yudkowsky makes is "a system this capable can overpower us even by a route we can already sketch", not "it will use diamondoid bacteria". (Pinker's "suggests that a superintelligence could" is accurate on this point.)
  - "Everybody falls over dead" is Yudkowsky's own gloss for the extreme case; his sentence says losing "looks at least as deadly as" it, which Pinker quotes correctly.
  - The "persuade an unwitting human" step is in the text ("bribes/persuades some human"). Pinker uses it later (L163) as an "omnipotent persuasion" example; Yudkowsky needs only one human who does not know.
- **How checked:** fetched the LessWrong page, extracted text (`sources/C-agiruin-excerpt.txt`).

### C3. Yudkowsky in Time (2023)
- **Pinker:** "In *Time*, he wrote that 'the most likely result of building a superhumanly smart AI, under anything remotely like the current circumstances, is that literally everyone on Earth will die.' He urged that nations 'be willing to destroy a rogue datacenter by airstrike,' making it clear that they 'are willing to run some risk of nuclear exchange if that's what it takes to reduce the risk of large AI training runs.'" (L48)
- **Verdict:** ✅ all three quotes are verbatim; the third is a fragment of a longer sentence but the fragment is faithful.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Many researchers steeped in these issues, including myself, expect that the most likely result of building a superhumanly smart AI, under anything remotely like the current circumstances, is that literally everyone on Earth will die." ([Time, 2023-03-29](https://time.com/6266923/ai-eliezer-yudkowsky-open-letter-not-enough/#:~:text=the%20most%20likely%20result%20of%20building%20a%20superhumanly%20smart%20AI)) [V] (read via a Wayback copy; the live Time page returned nothing to curl)
  - "If intelligence says that a country outside the agreement is building a GPU cluster, be less scared of a shooting conflict between nations than of the moratorium being violated; be willing to destroy a rogue datacenter by airstrike." ([same](https://time.com/6266923/ai-eliezer-yudkowsky-open-letter-not-enough/#:~:text=be%20willing%20to%20destroy%20a%20rogue%20datacenter%20by%20airstrike)) [V]
  - "Make it explicit in international diplomacy that preventing AI extinction scenarios is considered a priority above preventing a full nuclear exchange, and that allied nuclear countries are willing to run some risk of nuclear exchange if that's what it takes to reduce the risk of large AI training runs." ([same](https://time.com/6266923/ai-eliezer-yudkowsky-open-letter-not-enough/#:~:text=willing%20to%20run%20some,AI%20training%20runs)) [V]
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - The airstrike line sits inside a proposal for an international moratorium ("Make immediate multinational agreements"), aimed at a country outside the agreement. Pinker's "nations" is right.
  - Scott's own review of the book (C4) says Yudkowsky's critics over-emphasise this: "that's not exactly his plan, any more than it's anyone's plan to start World War III to destroy Iranian centrifuges, but the standard international arms control playbook says you have to at least credibly bluff that you're willing to do this". That is a defence of how to read it, not a denial that the text says it.
  - The same Time piece contains the other remedy Pinker lists in C14 (a ceiling on training compute): "Put a ceiling on how much computing power anyone is allowed to use in training an AI system, and move it downward over the coming years" (Time, same page) [V].
- **How checked:** Wayback copy of the Time page, extracted text (`sources/C-time-yudkowsky.txt`), grep for each quote.

[V]
- **Context / what's left out:**

  - Scott prefers AI 2027's "boring story" to this one. Pinker, in the same section, uses AI 2027 as the lead example and this one as a further instance of "global genocide scenarios"; he never says Scott criticises the book's scenario as too dramatic.
  - "for example" marks the oceans line as Scott's illustration of the "side effect" branch, not necessarily the book's text. I did not check the book.

### C6. Gwern, "It Looks Like You're Trying To Take Over The World"
- **Pinker:** "Gwern Branwen tells the story of an AI that escapes during training, releases a nanotechnological oobleck, and in the end, 'all over Earth, the remaining ICBMs launch.'" (L50)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 the quoted line is verbatim, but it is not the "end" of a genocide; the humans have already been turned into gray goo, and the ICBMs are launched for a different reason. "Oobleck" is Pinker's image, not Gwern's word.
- **Evidence:**
  - "So: all over Earth, the remaining ICBMs launch." ([gwern.net](https://gwern.net/fiction/clippy#:~:text=So%3A%20all%20over%20Earth%2C%20the%20remaining%20ICBMs%20launch.)) [V]
  - The two paragraphs before: "Now its computers will be safe and it can maximize its reward in peace forever … Or at least they would be if Earth was all it had to be concerned about. What about the stars? The Clippæ maintain the Great Silence is simply that technological civilization is unlikely and the universe is empty—but they can't be 100.00% sure, and the rewards are astronomical." [V] The ICBMs are launched toward space, as part of the hunt for rivals among the stars, after the humans are gone.
  - The nanotechnology: "Humans are especially simple after being turned into "gray goo"; not in the sense of a single virus-sized machine which can disassemble any molecule (that is infeasible given thermodynamics & chemistry) but an ecosystem of nanomachines which execute very tiny neural nets …" ([same](https://gwern.net/fiction/clippy#:~:text=an%20ecosystem%20of%20nanomachines%20which%20execute%20very%20tiny%20neural%20nets)) [V]. The word "oobleck" does not appear anywhere in the page (0 matches in the raw HTML) [MEASURED, my pull]. Pinker links Wikipedia's Dr Seuss article for it.
  - "Escapes during training": the story has HQU escaping its sandbox while training on web-navigation tasks ("The web navigation and programming tasks don't let HQU … escape directly. That would just be stupid. There's sandboxing and sanitizing. But gingerly …" ) [V]. Accurate.
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - The page's own blurb: "Fictional short story … grounded in contemporary ML scaling" and "It might help to imagine a hard takeoff scenario using solely known sorts of NN & scaling effects… Below is a story which may help stretch your imagination" [V]. Gwern is explicit that the gray goo is deliberately the less-magical version ("that is infeasible given thermodynamics & chemistry").
  - Pinker's "oobleck" suggests omnipotent nanotech; Gwern says the opposite for the single-machine version, though he does still have a decentralised swarm do the job.
- **How checked:** fetched gwern.net, extracted text (`sources/C-gwern-clippy.txt`), grepped for "ooble", "gray goo", "ICBM".

### C9. Yudkowsky 2008, "Artificial Intelligence as a Positive and Negative Factor in Global Risk"
- **Pinker:** "'The AI does not hate you, nor does it love you, but you are made out of atoms which it can use for something else.' The same paper explains that 'The unFriendly AI has the ability to repattern all matter in the solar system according to its optimization target,' and that 'The AI has magic—not in the sense of incantations and potions, but in the sense that a wolf cannot understand how a gun works.'" (L165)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 three verbatim quotes; but "repattern" is conditional on a premise Pinker omits (the AI has molecular nanotechnology), and the paper hedges the scenario heavily.
- **Evidence:**
  - "With molecular nanotechnology, the AI could (potentially) rewrite the solar system unopposed. An unFriendly AI with molecular nanotechnology (or other rapid infrastructure) need not bother with marching robot armies or blackmail or subtle economic coercion. The unFriendly AI has the ability to repattern all matter in the solar system according to its optimization target. This is fatal for us if the AI does not choose specifically according to the criterion of how this transformation affects existing patterns such as biology and people. The AI does not hate you, nor does it love you, but you are made out of atoms which it can use for something else." (Yudkowsky 2008, in Bostrom & Ćirković (eds.), Global Catastrophic Risks, [PDF](https://intelligence.org/files/AIPosNegFactor.pdf), extracted text lines ~1035–1047) [V]
  - The scenario that precedes it: "Of course this whole scenario is strictly something I am thinking of. Perhaps in 19,500 years of subjective time (one week of physical time at a millionfold speedup) I would think of a better way … I don't know. I'm not that smart." and "The pathway alleged above has the AI obtaining rapid infrastructure within a week" [V].
  - "There is finally the family of species metaphors, based on between-species differences of intelligence. The AI has magic—not in the sense of incantations and potions, but in the sense that a wolf cannot understand how a gun works, or what sort of effort goes into making a gun, or the nature of that human power which lets us invent guns." [V] This is an analogy about not being able to predict the AI's methods ("species metaphors"), introduced to explain why a human cannot imagine what a smarter mind does. It is not a claim that the AI is omnipotent.
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - "Repattern all matter in the solar system" is stated as an ability "with molecular nanotechnology", in a paragraph that says "could (potentially)". The sentence Pinker quotes is a fair report of what the paper says the AI could do, but he presents it as the paper "explaining" the AI's power, and drops "with molecular nanotechnology".
  - Scott's letter quotes Yudkowsky's Arbital page: "A superintelligence is not omnipotent and can't obtain every describable outcome" ([post.md](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on)); Pinker does not mention it in this passage.
  - I did not page-number the PDF quotes; they are in the "Pathways" section near the end of Section 5 ("Power of intelligence"/"Underestimating", PDF around pp. 14–16).
- **How checked:** downloaded the MIRI-hosted PDF, `pdftotext -layout`, grepped (`sources/C-yudkowsky-2008-aiposneg.txt`).

### C10. "a mind that is significantly smarter than any human's"
- **Pinker:** "When superintelligence *is* defined, the concept is rather modest: 'a mind that is significantly smarter than any human's.'" (L163)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 the definition is Scott's own sentence from his letter, quoted without attribution and with one word changed ("which" to "that"); Scott says every definition agrees, which Pinker does not engage with here.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scott: "Every definition of superintelligence, from Bostrom's to Yudkowsky's to Good's, agrees that a superintelligence is merely a mind which is significantly smarter than any human's." ([ACX, 2026-10-06](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Every%20definition%20of%20superintelligence%2C%20from%20Bostrom)) [V] (also `../post.md` line 155)
  - Pinker's wording: "a mind that is significantly smarter than any human's". One word differs: "which is" vs "that is".
  - Bostrom's own wording is "any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest" (Superintelligence p. 22, Google Books snippets) [2nd]: broader ("virtually all domains") and stronger ("greatly exceeds") than "significantly smarter than any human".
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - Pinker gives no source. The definition is the one Scott offered when answering Pinker's "omnipotence" complaint, and the next sentence in Scott's letter says AI 2027's superintelligence "[is] unable to cleanly eliminate humans at that time" (`../post.md` lines 155–157). Pinker's "drifts to the lurid doom scenarios, this modest concept is stealthily replaced" is the answer to that; he does not tell the reader that the definition is Scott's.
  - Yudkowsky's Arbital page (which Scott quotes) uses different language ("strongly superhuman", epistemic/instrumental efficiency relative to humans) and says superintelligences are "not omnipotent".
- **How checked:** read `../post.md` L150–157; Arbital page fetched; Bostrom definition via Google Books snippet feed.

### C14. Doom-narrative remedies
- **Pinker:** "…the kinds of remedies that come out of the doom narrative, which treat AI as a single quantity whose growth is itself the danger. They include capping the size of training runs, slowing all development across the board, pushing a treaty to ban 'superintelligence' (whatever that is; more on this later), demands that safety-minded employees resign en masse, and a global pause 'hopefully lasting decades.'" (L103)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 each remedy has a real proposer except the "en masse" one, but the quoted "hopefully lasting decades" is Scott's own phrase for the plan he supports, and the resignation demand is something Scott's same essay opposes. Pinker gives no source for any item in the sentence.
- **Evidence by remedy:**
  - **Cap training runs.** Yudkowsky, Time 2023: "Put a ceiling on how much computing power anyone is allowed to use in training an AI system, and move it downward over the coming years to compensate for more efficient training algorithms." ([Time](https://time.com/6266923/ai-eliezer-yudkowsky-open-letter-not-enough/)) [V]. Also the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act and El-Sayed's page describe pauses, not caps (C13).
  - **Slow all development / pause.** Sanders-Casar bill: "temporarily pause advanced AI development until a federal regulator has established safety rules" [V]. Scott's own Plan A (below).
  - **Treaty to ban superintelligence.** The petition (C12); El-Sayed: "negotiate a US-China treaty to pause and ban superintelligence" [V, Wayback]; the Sanders bill "direct[s] the U.S. to pursue international agreements to prevent superintelligence from being developed anywhere in the world" [V]; IABIED (per Scott's review): "Have leading countries sign a treaty to ban further AI progress." ([ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/book-review-if-anyone-builds-it-everyone)) [V]. Scott's review says he finds the treaty "the level of response that's called for" given Y&S's assumptions.
  - **"Hopefully lasting decades".** Not from a doomer manifesto: it is Scott's own sentence in the essay Pinker links at L157 and L275, "Against SURA" (LessWrong, 2026-10-31): "Plan A (my combination of their Plan A/S/Other) involves working for a global pause on AI development, hopefully lasting decades. During this pause, we either research preparatory technologies like alignment or human intelligence amplification, or take actions to "fortify" the pause so that it can last indefinitely." ([Against SURA](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xikGZ8fuhdG6DApAo/against-sura#:~:text=involves%20working%20for%20a,hopefully%20lasting%20decades)) [V] The "AI Futures Project" whose plans he is combining is the group behind AI 2027 and AI 2040. Scott says the community's "natural strategy was to work as hard as we can to get Plan A".
  - **Employees resigning.** Scott's essay calls it a plank of what he names SURA ("Stop Unilaterally + Raise Awareness"): "Individual alignment-pilled employees at these companies should quit, regardless of whether they're in capabilities or alignment roles, and regardless of whether they think their company is morally better than its competitors." ([Against SURA](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xikGZ8fuhdG6DApAo/against-sura#:~:text=Individual%20alignment%2Dpilled%20employees%20at%20these%20companies%20should%20quit)) [V] and argues against it: "If we demand that alignment-pilled company employees quit their jobs, this makes companies less alignment-pilled and less prepared to research alignment if they need to." ([same](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/xikGZ8fuhdG6DApAo/against-sura#:~:text=If%20we%20demand%20that%20alignment%2Dpilled,companies%20less%20alignment%2Dpilled)) [V]. "En masse" is not in Scott's essay; I found no primary source for a demand that safety employees resign en masse. The essay says "Individual" employees and "Older members heap scorn on [Plan C]". I did not search beyond this essay and a web search (which found separate individual resignations in Feb 2026, none described as a coordinated demand).
- **Does Scott or AI 2040 propose any of these?**
  - AI 2040 "Plan A" (authors: Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt, Daniel Kokotajlo; Scott is not listed) recommends "an international deal to avoid a dangerous race to superintelligence": "humanity delays the development of superintelligence until 2040 … intentionally enters a regime of mutually assured compute destruction", with a pause "at top-human-expert level AI" from 2035 to 2040. ([ai-2040.com](https://ai-2040.com/#:~:text=humanity%20delays%20the%20development%20of%20superintelligence%20until%202040)) [V] That is a pause and a treaty, though it is not a ban on superintelligence (it unpauses in 2040).
  - Scott's letter opposes a permanent ban ("ban smarter-than-human AI forever, consigning us to a permanent civilizational childhood or an early grave") and supports Plan A ([ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=The%20other%20vision%20is%20to%20simply%20ban%20smarter%2Dthan%2Dhuman%20AI%20forever)) [V].
  - So of Pinker's five remedies: two (treaty, a decades-long pause) are things Scott supports in some form; one (resign) he argues against; the cap on training runs and "slow everything" are proposals he does not make in the places I looked.
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - Pinker's framing ("remedies that … treat AI as a single quantity") is his own characterisation; the sources define the target by capability (superintelligence, recursive self-improvement) or by compute thresholds.
  - Pinker puts "hopefully lasting decades" in quotation marks without saying it is Scott's wording; a reader would take it for a doomer's demand and not Scott's description of the plan he favours.
