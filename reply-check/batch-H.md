### H2. "There is no Aviation Safety Community or Fire Protection Culture"
- **Pinker:** "as far as I know, there is no Aviation Safety Community or Fire Protection Culture." (L261)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 The point (safety fields are not ideological fraternities) is arguable, but the literal claim is wrong: both fields have named professional communities, and "safety culture" is a studied term.
- **Evidence:**
  - "The Flight Safety Foundation (FSF) is a nonprofit, international organization concerning research, education, advocacy, and communications in the field of aviation safety. FSF brings together aviation professionals" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_Safety_Foundation#:~:text=Flight%20Safety%20Foundation)) [V].
  - "The Society of Fire Protection Engineers (SFPE) has been a prominent professional organization since its establishment in 1950" ([SFPE](https://www.sfpe.org/about#:~:text=has%20been%20a%20prominent,establishment%20in%201950)) [V]. NFPA: "devoted to eliminating death, injury, property damage, and economic loss due to fire" with 50,000 members, per Wikipedia [V].
  - Wikipedia, "Safety culture": "Safety culture is the element of organizational culture which is concerned with the maintenance of safety and compliance with safety standards." [V]
  - For Pinker: those communities are guilds of engineers with regulators and standards, which is his next sentence ("not a guild of safety engineers"). The contrast he is drawing is real; the "no such community" phrasing is not.
- **How checked:** Wikipedia and SFPE fetched; NFPA page loaded but its text did not match my passage.

### H3. Newport's quotes, and the "Anthropic statement"
- **Pinker:** "As Newport puts it, the labs' leaders hope 'to summon the digital deity of superintelligent AI which, in their futurist eschatology, will either heal the world or destroy it,' and that 'they see themselves as prophets attempting to usher in a new Messianic age.'" (L263)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Both quotes are verbatim, but Newport hedged them ("if I were to guess", "In some sense") and wrote them about the companies' agent research, not "the labs' leaders hope".
- **Evidence:**
  - "I'm not certain of the answer, but if I were to guess, it probably has a lot to do with the Silicon Valley technological salvation ideology (to borrow a term from Adam Becker) that heavily influenced key figures like Sam Altman and Dario Amodei, as well as many of their employees. They think these types of agents are their best bet to [summon the digital deity of superintelligent AI which, in their futurist eschatology, will either heal the world or destroy it](https://calnewport.com/anthropic-just-threatened-to-kill-billions-of-people-this-is-not-okay/#:~:text=summon%20the%20digital%20deity,or%20destroy%20it). In some sense, they see themselves as prophets attempting to usher in a new Messianic age." (Newport, Sept 9 2026) [V] Pinker drops "In some sense" and the "I'm not certain ... guess" frame.
  - The thing "they" are summoning is, in Newport's text, a specific kind of system: "long-horizon, dangerously equipped unsupervised LLM-powered agents". Newport also writes "I don't believe that LLM-powered agents, on their own, can become superintelligent and create existential risk" [V]. So his target is reckless agent experiments, not a view that doom is a cult belief.
- **What the Anthropic statement was:** Newport's title ("Anthropic Just Threatened to Kill Billions of People. This Is Not Okay.") refers to tweets by two Anthropic employees, not a company statement, after researcher Jacob Coxon resigned on Sept 8 saying "[OpenAI and Anthropic] are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives":
  - Evan Hubinger, head of Alignment Science: "[Jacob is correct here—we really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans!](https://calnewport.com/anthropic-just-threatened-to-kill-billions-of-people-this-is-not-okay/#:~:text=we%20really%20do%20earnestly%20believe%20AI%20could%20kill%20all%20humans) I personally think it is >10% within the next decade." [V, as quoted by Newport]
  - Samuel Marks: "AI developers believe their technology could cause human extinction ... In general, the more senior the employee, the more concerned they are." ([post](https://calnewport.com/anthropic-just-threatened-to-kill-billions-of-people-this-is-not-okay/#:~:text=In%20general%2C%20the%20more,concerned%20they%20are)) [V, as quoted by Newport]. Goldberg's column quotes the same Coxon and Hubinger tweets [V].
  - Newport's follow-up the next day: "Yesterday, multiple Anthropic employees casually announced online that they thought the types of AI they're building could kill billions of people." ([post](https://calnewport.com/superintelligence-is-a-fairy-tale-but-chasing-it-can-still-cause-harm/)) [V]. I did not pull the original tweets from scry.
- **Context / what's left out:** Newport's reading is that these employees are *sincere* believers. That cuts against Pinker's line in section XIII that the labs' alarm is self-interested "doom-trolling". Both come from Newport (see H8).
- **How checked:** fetched both Newport posts (dates in page metadata: Sept 9 and 10, 2026), Goldberg syndicated copy.

### H4. Lehmann in The Dispatch, and the Catholic Church
- **Pinker:** "Not coincidentally, suggests Claire Lehmann, the Catholic Church, which 'has some experience with millenarian cults,' has emerged as a major critic of AI doom." (L263)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 The words are Lehmann's (her subtitle). But what the Church is criticising, in her piece and in the encyclical, is Anthropic's consciousness claims and transhumanism, not extinction risk. The encyclical itself warns about an AI arms race.
- **Evidence:**
  - Dispatch, Oct 7 2026, ["Why the Catholic Church Is AI Doom's Most Formidable Foe"](https://web.archive.org/web/20261019052412/https://thedispatch.com/article/catholic-church-artificial-intelligence-millenarian/#:~:text=The%20church%20has%20some%20experience%20with%20millenarian%20cults), subtitle: "The church has some experience with millenarian cults." [V] Paywalled; I read the lede from the Wayback copy and the long excerpt on [TaxProf Blog](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2026/10/18/why-the-catholic-church-is-ai-dooms-most-formidable-foe/#:~:text=had%20politely%20refused%20to%20concede%20that%20large%20language%20models) (the excerpt has "…" gaps; the full article was not read).
  - Her argument, per the excerpt: Olah "nearly boycotted the historic event over a profound disagreement. The pope ... had politely refused to concede that large language models, or LLMs, were on the cusp of achieving consciousness." [V] The Pope's cited tweet is about art and "Algorithms lack the spark of humanity." The company at issue is Anthropic, "and it released its constitution ... 'Claude might have some kind of consciousness or moral status'." [V]
  - The millenarian-cult analogy: "Anthropic's leaders—and the effective altruist community that surrounds them—cast themselves as the custodians of humanity's future... Like the millenarian movements described by Cohn, they believe history is approaching a turning point" [V, excerpt]. Her ending: Leo "rejected them because the Catholic Church knows a millenarian cult when it sees one." [V, excerpt] Her own causal claim is about *consciousness*, a speculation about the Pope's motive.
  - The encyclical *Magnifica humanitas*, dated May 15, released May 25 2026: I searched the full text. "extinction", "apocalypse", "superintelligence", "catastrophe", "doom" occur 0 times [V; grep of [vatican.va text](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html)]. It does criticise [transhumanism and posthumanism](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html#:~:text=transhumanism%20and%20posthumanism) as "the ideological background present in some centers of technological power" [V], and ¶110: "[Disarming AI means freeing it from the mentality of 'armed' competition](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html#:~:text=Disarming%20AI%20means%20freeing%20it%20from%20the%20mentality%20of) ... This entails [a race for ever more powerful algorithms and larger datasets](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html#:~:text=a%20race%20for%20ever%20more%20powerful%20algorithms%20and%20larger%20datasets)" [V].
  - Olah did attend and nearly pulled out ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnifica_humanitas#:~:text=Olah%20considered%20pulling%20Anthropic%20from%20the%20presentation), citing NYT and Fortune) [2nd].
- **Context / what's left out:** "Critic of AI doom" is the article's title framing. The Pope is not arguing that extinction scenarios are false; he is rejecting machine consciousness, transhumanist aims and power concentration. The encyclical shares some concerns with the doomers (an AI race with no brakes). Lehmann is also someone Scott attacked by name (L-section XIII), so Pinker is citing his opponent's target; Lehmann's piece is an op-ed, not research.
- **How checked:** Dispatch (Wayback + TaxProf excerpt), Vatican text grep, Wikipedia.

### H5. Funders and gift sizes; "Doing EA Better"
- **Pinker:** "[The culture's] institutions have become the philanthropies of choice of many tech billionaires, including Sam Bankman-Fried, Dustin Moskovitz and Cari Tuna, Jaan Tallinn, and Vitalik Buterin, who have showered them with nine-figure gifts." (L265)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Buterin's gift and Moskovitz and Tuna's giving clearly hit nine figures. For Tallinn it holds only as a cumulative total of his fund, and SBF's is a total of commitments. And the biggest funder's money mostly went to global health, not to the "culture's" AI institutions.
- **Evidence:**
  - Buterin: Future of Life Institute (FLI) received a 2021 SHIB donation; FLI told Politico "The value of that gift ultimately came to $665.8 million" [2nd; Politico 403, via search snippet]. Buterin has said it brought in "something like $500M" [2nd]. Nine figures, single gift.
  - Moskovitz and Tuna: via Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving), "directed over $1 billion in 2025, the most in our history" ([2025 CEO letter, p. 1](https://coefficientgiving.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-Letter-from-the-CEO-final.pdf#page=1)) [V]. The July 2026 GiveWell commitment alone is [$1 billion](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/increasing-our-2026-allocation-to-givewells-recommendations-to-1-billion/#:~:text=we%E2%80%99re%20now%20increasing%20that%20commitment%20to%20%241%20billion%20for%202026) [V]. See H9: most of their money goes to non-AI causes.
  - Tallinn: Survival and Flourishing Fund (SFF), which his money mainly backs, ["has organized ~$216MM in philanthropic gifts and grants"](https://survivalandflourishing.fund/#:~:text=SFF%20has%20organized%20~%24216MM%20in%20philanthropic%20gifts%20and%20grants) since 2019, annual peak $41MM (2024) [V]. So nine figures cumulative, not a nine-figure gift. (SFF 2026 also includes Moskovitz money, per search snippet [2nd].) Tallinn also led Anthropic's Series A [2nd, Dario Said].
  - SBF: FTX Future Fund "committed around $160 million to 110 nonprofits" by Sept 2022 [2nd, Wikipedia/Fortune via search], and about $500M of Anthropic equity (an investment, not a gift) [2nd]. Nine figures in aggregate.
- **"Doing EA Better" quote:** [verbatim and correctly attributed](https://web.archive.org/web/2026/https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/54vAiSFkYszTWWWv4/doing-ea-better-1#:~:text=align%20remarkably%20well%20with,most%20concerned%20about): "It is worth noting that the areas EA focuses on most intensely (the long-term future and existential risk, and especially AI risk within that) align remarkably well with the sorts of things tech billionaires are most concerned about: longtermism is the closest thing to 'doing sci-fi in real life', existential catastrophes are one of the few ways in which wealthy people could come to harm, and AI is the threat most interesting to people who made their fortunes in computing." [V] It is the section titled "We align suspiciously well with the interests of tech billionaires (and ourselves)", signed by "ConcernedEAs", posted January 2023 (page date). "A team of dissident EAs" is fair. Pinker's version stops before "sci-fi in real life" and omits the response in the comments: "I just don't think it is true that we align well with the interests of tech billionaires. We've managed to persuade two billionaires of EA" [V, top comment].
- **Context / what's left out:** SBF's money is gone: Open Philanthropy's own framing is that, after the FTX collapse, "we raised our cost-effectiveness bar for GCR spending" ([2023 progress post](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/our-progress-in-2023-and-plans-for-2024/)) [V]. The Doing EA Better post predates the 2024-26 growth in Coefficient's non-AI giving.
- **How checked:** fetched Wayback copy of the EA Forum post (live page 429s), SFF homepage, Coefficient letters; Politico/FTX figures are secondary.

- **Pinker:** "the expert consensus Alexander invokes is, to a large extent, the common creed of a single, well-funded intellectual community." (L267)
- **Verdict:** ❌ for the survey-based part of Scott's consensus, 🟡 for the named individuals. The researchers Scott cites were mostly not products of the rationalist/EA community, and the survey he cites sampled ML publishers.
- **Evidence:**
  - What Scott invoked ([post](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on)): "the average AI researcher in Grace's 1,600 person sample said 18%", Hinton "10-50%", Amodei "10-25%", Musk "10-30%" [V, post.md L129]; "Hinton ... Bengio ... Ilya Sutskever ... Paul Christiano" as eminent researchers (L179).
  - The survey: the 2024 Expert Survey on Progress in AI recruited "participants who had published in 2023 at any of ... NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, JMLR" and more [V, [docs/ESPAI2024](../docs/ESPAI2024%20-%20Grace%20et%20al%202026.html)]. ### H7. Cohn quote, SBF, and the Asterisk survey
- **Pinker:** Cohn: "a restlessly dynamic and utterly ruthless group which, obsessed by the apocalyptic phantasy ..." SBF: "framed his drive for massive wealth as an 'earning to give' strategy ... he ended up in prison for one of the largest financial frauds in history." Asterisk: "the Rationalist Community has spawned about half a dozen cult-like groups, one of which has been 'linked to six violent deaths.'" (L291)
- **Verdict:** ✅ on the quotes; 🟡 on how Cohn and Asterisk are used.
- **Evidence:**
  - Cohn, ch. "Conclusion" of [The Pursuit of the Millennium](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/norman-cohn-the-pursuit-of-the-millennium#:~:text=a%20restlessly%20dynamic%20and%20utterly%20ruthless%20group): "And what emerged then was a new group — a restlessly dynamic and utterly ruthless group which, obsessed by the apocalyptic phantasy and filled with the conviction of its own infallibility, set itself infinitely above the rest of humanity and recognized no claims save that of its own supposed mission. And finally this group might — though it did not always — succeed in imposing its leadership on the great mass of the disoriented." [V] Pinker's "recognised" is British spelling of the same word. Cohn's "group" forms around a *propheta* (usually a freelance ex-priest) amid social upheaval among the poor. Pinker's "the apocalyptic movements of medieval Europe produced" generalises: Cohn says "might ... though it did not always".
  - SBF: DOJ sentencing release: Williams said Bankman-Fried "orchestrated one of the largest financial frauds in history" ([SDNY, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261113045538/https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/samuel-bankman-fried-sentenced-25-years-prison#:~:text=one%20of%20the%20largest%20financial%20frauds%20in%20history)) [V]. Sentence 25 years; appeal upheld June 2026 [2nd]. The WaPo piece Pinker links (Oct 2023 opinion) was blocked (403), so I did not read its "earning to give" framing.
  - Asterisk: "the rationalist community has hosted perhaps half a dozen small groups with very strange beliefs ... Some ... seem to have caused no harm but bad takes. But the most famous, a loose group of vegan anarchist transhumanists nicknamed the Zizians, have been [linked to six violent deaths](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/11/why-are-there-so-many-rationalist-cults#:~:text=have%20been%20linked%20to%20six%20violent%20deaths)." ([Asterisk issue 11](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/11/why-are-there-so-many-rationalist-cults#:~:text=perhaps%20half%20a%20dozen%20small%20groups%20with%20very%20strange%20beliefs)) [V] Pinker's "spawned about half a dozen cult-like groups" is stronger than "hosted ... small groups with very strange beliefs", and he drops that some caused "no harm but bad takes". The author is "a card-carrying Rat", so "insider" is right. The article also says Yudkowsky "shows little interest in running" a cult and "discourages social isolation" [V].
- **How checked:** Asterisk, Cohn (full text copy), DOJ via Wayback.

### H8. Newport quotes, the Ford analogy, and the 1990s biotech history
- **Pinker:** Newport "When you believe, as many at OpenAI and Anthropic reportedly do, that humanity's fate rests on winning a race ..."; "this behavior was negligent. But they likely justified it because it served the far grander goal of summoning a digital God"; the Ford F-150 analogy; the biotech passage "When the American biotech industry became worried about the negative potential of genetic engineering tools in the 1990s, it didn't rush ahead to clone humans before China could get there; it instead lobbied Congress and relevant international bodies ..." (L295-301)
- **Verdict:** ✅ the two Newport blog quotes are verbatim; 🟡 the NYT Ford/biotech block (not independently readable; Newport's blog confirms the Ford idea but not Pinker's wording); 🟡 the biotech history is half right.
- **Evidence:**
  - "[When you believe, as many at OpenAI and Anthropic reportedly do,](https://calnewport.com/superintelligence-is-a-fairy-tale-but-chasing-it-can-still-cause-harm/#:~:text=as%20many%20at%20OpenAI%20and%20Anthropic%20reportedly%20do) that humanity's fate rests on winning a race to create the most powerful technology ever, you tend to care less about the damage you inflict along the way." [V] Also verbatim: "[this behavior was negligent. But they likely justified it](https://calnewport.com/superintelligence-is-a-fairy-tale-but-chasing-it-can-still-cause-harm/#:~:text=this%20behavior%20was%20negligent.%20But%20they%20likely%20justified%20it)". Pinker's "The Hugging Face incident" matches Newport's "HuggingFace hacking attacks from this summer".
  - Cut from the second quote: the next parenthesis "Clearly, there may be additional motivations at play here as well. Given OpenAI's interest in an IPO ... there might have been pressure to do well on this particular benchmark" and "the more I talk to sources connected to these companies, the more I've come to believe that their commitment to futurist ideas is what really drives a lot of their more irresponsible behavior" [V]. Newport also concludes the harm is "more cyber mayhem than catastrophic risk" and that Anthropic and OpenAI are "not able to create superintelligent AI" [V]. Pinker's use as an argument against *safety* motives is fair; Newport's piece is an argument that superintelligence is "a Fairy Tale".
  - Ford analogy: Newport's [blog post on his NYT op-ed](https://calnewport.com/dear-ai-companies-stop-the-doom-trolling/#:~:text=The%20Ford%20Motor%20Company%20releases%20a%20slick%20whitepaper) ("Imagine, for a moment ... The Ford Motor Company releases a slick whitepaper making the alarming claim that they're concerned their popular F-150 pickup trucks might soon spontaneously burst into flames", ending "this 'possible future' would be bad, but that there's nothing they can do about the issue so long as 'less cautious' automobile companies exist") [V]. It says the op-ed ran in NYT online "last week" and a commenter gives "June 17, 2026" [V]. The op-ed's own title is "Dear A.I. Companies, the Doom Trolling Has to Stop" [V, blog]. Pinker's block quote is worded differently ("Imagine if Ford put out a report saying that it feared ... too inevitable and important to slow down. ### H10. Pinker's description of longtermism versus Greaves and MacAskill
- **Pinker:** "If human extinction would pre-empt the existence of trillions of future people (indeed, of an even greater number of disembodied consciousnesses), then even a minuscule reduction in its probability outweighs any amount of good done for people alive today. This includes the mitigation of suffering today from famines, disease, and climate catastrophes as long as they fall short of extinguishing the species outright." (L307)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 A fair gist of the headline numbers and "digital" beings, but overstated: Greaves and MacAskill make a comparative claim about the "most important decision situations", say near-term benefits are not small, flag tiny probabilities as the main way their argument could fail, and the thesis is about the far future, not only extinction.
- **Evidence** ([Greaves and MacAskill, "The Case for Strong Longtermism", GPI working paper 2021](../Greaves-MacAskill-Case-for-Strong-Longtermism-2021.pdf)):
  - The thesis: "[Axiological strong longtermism (ASL)](../docs/Greaves-MacAskill-Case-for-Strong-Longtermism-2021.html#:~:text=Axiological%20strong%20longtermism%20%28ASL%29): In the most important decision situations facing agents today, (i) Every option that is near-best overall is near-best for the far future. (ii) Every option that is near-best overall delivers much larger benefits in the far future than in the near future." [V, PDF p. 4]
  - [ASL makes only comparative claims](../docs/Greaves-MacAskill-Case-for-Strong-Longtermism-2021.html#:~:text=ASL%20makes%20only%20comparative%20claims): "We do not claim, and nor do we believe, that options cannot deliver large benefits without being near-best for the far future, or that available near-future benefits are small in any absolute sense. Our claim is rather that available benefits for the far future are many times larger even than this." [V, PDF p. 5] This is the opposite of "outweighs any amount of good".
  - The size of the "minuscule" reduction: "a reduction in near-term risk of extinction by one millionth of one percentage point would be equivalent in value to a million lives saved; on our main estimate of 10^24 expected future lives, this becomes ten quadrillion (10^16) lives saved." [V, [p. 12](../Greaves-MacAskill-Case-for-Strong-Longtermism-2021.pdf#page=12)] So Pinker's gist matches the paper's own illustration. They use antimalarial bednets as "an approximate upper bound on attainable near-future benefits per unit of spending" [V, p. 2].
  - Digital minds: "digital sentience could dramatically increase the number of beings who could live around one star" and they run a "digital life" scenario with 10^34-10^45 lives [V, section 3]. Pinker's "disembodied consciousnesses" is a fair gloss.
  - Their caveat on tiny probabilities: "[We regard this as one of the most plausible ways in which the argument for strong longtermism might fail](../docs/Greaves-MacAskill-Case-for-Strong-Longtermism-2021.html#:~:text=We%20regard%20this%20as,which%20the%20argument%20for)... research into the possibility of a non-fanatical decision theory is currently embryonic" [V, PDF p. 26]. They also argue "the probabilities involved ... might not be sufficiently extreme" to matter. The paper's examples include asteroid detection and pandemic preparedness, with AI safety, in order to claim tractability [V].
  - Not only extinction: the paper also argues for improving the "trajectory" of civilisation, not just avoiding extinction ("we aim to make the trajectory of that civilisation better") [V]. Pinker's "as long as they fall short of extinguishing the species" omits trajectory changes.
- **Context / what's left out:** Pinker is describing "longtermism", not the paper. The paper is the most-cited academic statement of the *strong* version; weaker versions (MacAskill's *What We Owe the Future*) are a different claim. I did not check that book.
- **How checked:** read the whole PDF text (pdftotext), local HTML in `docs/`.
