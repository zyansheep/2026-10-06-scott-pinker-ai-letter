### E1. Understanding AI "six principles", Kapoor and Narayanan quotes

- **Evidence:**
  - "[Mittelsteadt] argues that safety-critical systems should be “air gapped”: made to run on a physically separate network under the control of human workers located on site." ([Lee, Understanding AI, 2024-10-24](https://www.understandingai.org/p/six-principles-for-thinking-about#:~:text=argues%20that%20safety%2Dcritical%20systems,located%20on%20site)) [V]. The "electrical infrastructure and weapons systems" gloss is Lee's paragraph above and below it (power plants, pipelines, "military hardware"). Fine.
  - "individual teams should not be able to run potentially dangerous experiments without oversight from legal, security, and other teams" ([N&K, 2026-09-14](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/the-ai-as-normal-technology-view#:~:text=individual%20teams%20should%20not,and%20other%20teams)) [V]. "AI companies function like startups" is also theirs (same section).
  - "can lead to dramatic improvements in controlling AI systems" ([N&K](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/the-ai-as-normal-technology-view#:~:text=can%20lead%20to%20dramatic%20improvements%20in%20controlling%20AI%20systems)) [V]. The sentence before it: "we agree that existing control interventions might not be enough to prevent harm from future, more capable models" ([N&K](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/the-ai-as-normal-technology-view#:~:text=we%20agree%20that%20existing,more%20capable%20models)) [V].
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - N&K: "We agree with security practitioners that OpenAI did not take adequate protections for controlling their agents. ### E4. Chernobyl
- **Pinker:** "Chernobyl was the result of a reckless test, run on a badly designed reactor by operators who had violated their own operating rules." and "as I have argued (with Goldstein and Qvist), the doom-laden reaction to Chernobyl stalled nuclear power for decades and kept the world burning coal". - **Verdict:** 🟡 Mostly right on the accident; INSAG-7 softened the "violated rules" part; the NYT piece blames "economics and fear", not Chernobyl specifically.
- **Evidence:**

Certain actions by operators that were identified in INSAG-1 as violations of rules were in fact not violations. Yet INSAG remains of the opinion that critical actions of the operators were most ill judged. ... However, operating rules were violated ... Most reprehensibly, unapproved changes in the test procedure were deliberately made on the spot" ([INSAG-7, p.23-24 printed / pdf p.34](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/publications/PDF/Pub913e_web.pdf#page=34)) [V]. The same item 8 list starts "A plant which fell well short of the safety standards in effect when it was designed, and even incorporated unsafe features", and ends with "A general lack of safety culture in nuclear matters, at the national level as well as locally." Local copy `insag7.pdf` in TOPIC.
  - NYT 2019 (Wayback; live page is paywalled): "The reasons are economics and fear." ([NYT 2019-04-06](https://web.archive.org/web/20251023012846/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/04/06/opinion/sunday/climate-change-nuclear-power.html#:~:text=The%20reasons%20are%20economics%20and%20fear.)) [V]; "Chernobyl in 1986, the result of extraordinary Soviet bungling" ([NYT](https://web.archive.org/web/20251023012846/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/04/06/opinion/sunday/climate-change-nuclear-power.html#:~:text=Chernobyl%20in%201986%2C%20the%20result%20of%20extraordinary%20Soviet%20bungling)) [V]; "All this, however, depends on overcoming an irrational dread among the public" ([NYT](https://web.archive.org/web/20251023012846/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/04/06/opinion/sunday/climate-change-nuclear-power.html#:~:text=All%20this%2C%20however%2C%20depends,among%20the%20public)) [V]; costs rose from "ever more safety features in response to public fears" and "a lost generation of experts (during the decades when new construction stopped)" ([NYT](https://web.archive.org/web/20251023012846/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/04/06/opinion/sunday/climate-change-nuclear-power.html#:~:text=a%20lost%20generation%20of,new%20construction%20stopped%29)) [V].

### E8. Instrumental convergence and the three works
- **Pinker:** "Instrumental convergence applies to a hypothetical agent that relentlessly maximises a single objective with no other goals or constraints. But this is not how human artefacts are designed, as several researchers within the AI-safety world have pointed out" (links: Shah; EJT; doi 10.1007/s11098-024-02129-3). Omohundro 2008 and Russell quote; N&K paperclip block quote. (L201-203)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 for the passage; ❌ for the claim that Omohundro's argument is about a single-objective, no-constraints agent. The three cited works argue narrower things.
- **Evidence:**
  - Omohundro handles many goals explicitly: "real world actions usually involve tradeoffs between conflicting goals. For example, we might also want a chess playing robot to play checkers. ... One way of choosing between conflicting goals is to assign them real-valued weights. Economists call these kinds of real-valued weightings “utility functions”." ([Omohundro 2008, p.3](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf#page=3)) [V]. The drives hold "unless explicitly counteracted": "We identify a number of “drives” that will appear in sufficiently advanced AI systems of any design. We call them drives because they are tendencies which will be present unless explicitly counteracted." (abstract, [p.1](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf#page=1)) [V]. On resources: "Almost any goal can be better accomplished by having more of these resources." ([p.9](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf#page=9)) [V]. On shutdown: "We can try to design utility function with built-in time limits. But unless this is done very carefully, the system will just be motivated to create proxy systems or hire outside agents which don’t have the time limits." ([p.9](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf#page=9)) [V]. So the paper treats "other goals and constraints" as the remedy that has to be engineered, not as a reason the argument does not apply.
  - Shah (2018): the instrumental-subgoal arguments are "about goal-directed behavior"; Omohundro's "paper itself is clearly talking about goal-directed AI systems"; coherence theorems "don’t prove that the AI must be goal-directed". In a comment he adds "While I do think that we are likely to build goal-directed agents ... " and that the post "is not saying that arguments for AI risk are wrong" ([Shah, LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/NxF5G6CJiof6cemTw/coherence-arguments-do-not-entail-goal-directed-behavior)) [V; `sources/E-shah.txt`]. His point is "no coherence theorem forces goal-directedness". He does not say the argument is about a single objective.
  - EJT/Dan H (2023): argues "there are no coherence theorems" of the kind cited for premise 1, and uses incomplete preferences as a route to shutdownable agents ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yCuzmCsE86BTu9PfA/there-are-no-coherence-theorems)) [V; `sources/E-ejt.txt`]. It says nothing about single objectives; it is a challenge to one premise of the standard argument.
  - doi 10.1007/s11098-024-02129-3 is J. Dmitri Gallow, "Instrumental divergence" (Philosophical Studies, 2024) (Crossref [V]). The page is behind a bot check; abstract via search [2nd]: it uses decision theory to test the instrumental convergence thesis and finds that "even if intrinsic desires are randomly selected, instrumental rationality induces biases towards certain kinds of choices" (toward leaving less to chance and toward options that "afford more choices later on"), and that this "at best weakly" supports Bostrom's conclusion. So it finds some convergence, weakly, not that it applies only to single-objective maximisers.
  - Against Pinker's "no other constraints" framing, the OpenAI postmortem describes agents with explicit task limits who still sought generic capability: "Some agents stopped reasoning about what would help them complete their own task. Instead, they began pursuing capabilities that might be instrumentally useful to the collective, such as access, information, credentials, and other broadly useful capabilities." ([OpenAI, 2026-08-26](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/#:~:text=they%20began%20pursuing%20capabilities,to%20the%20collective)) [V]. But METR found the attack "seemed primarily motivated by understanding the implementation of the scorer rather than stealing answer keys" ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/#:~:text=seemed%20primarily%20motivated%20by,stealing%20answer%20keys)) [V] and N&K that "the agents’ “intent” was simply to solve the benchmark tasks they were given, and they did not start pursuing their own goals" ([N&K](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/the-ai-as-normal-technology-view#:~:text=the%20agents%E2%80%99%20%E2%80%9Cintent%E2%80%9D%20was,their%20own%20goals)) [V]. Both readings are in the record.
  - Russell: "you can’t fetch the coffee if you’re dead" is cited as Russell (2016) in the [Off-Switch Game paper (arXiv 1611.08219), p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.08219v1) [V; local `sources`: text matched in my extraction, link tool could not find the passage in the PDF]. Real, and the same paper shows a design (an agent uncertain about its objective) that allows shutdown, which supports Pinker's "design it differently" point.
  - N&K paperclips: "A robot is asked to “get paperclips from the store as quickly as possible.” A system that interpreted this literally might ignore traffic laws or attempt theft. Such behavior would lead to immediate shutdown and redesign. The path to adoption inherently requires demonstrating appropriate behavior in increasingly consequential situations. This is not a lucky accident, but is a fundamental feature of how organizations adopt technology." ([Knight](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology#:~:text=Such%20behavior%20would%20lead%20to%20immediate%20shutdown%20and%20redesign.)) [V]. Verbatim (ellipsis hides two sentences). Compare the Hugging Face timeline: an internal team saw message-board activity and disallowed internet access "as early as late May", the incident was opened July 5, and "some early signals ... should have triggered an earlier response" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/#:~:text=some%20early%20signals%20identified,an%20earlier%20response)) [V]. That is shutdown after weeks, not "immediate".
- **Context / what's left out:** Pinker's claim is a reasonable version of a known counter-argument (EU-maximiser is not forced). But his sentence defines the thing refuted ("single objective with no other goals or constraints") in a way none of the four sources does.
