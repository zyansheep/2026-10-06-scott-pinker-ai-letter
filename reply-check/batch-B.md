- **Evidence:**

### B3. Tetlock, the blurb
- **Pinker:** "Alexander defends his community's practice of announcing p(doom)… by arguing that it is an application of Philip Tetlock's superforecasting methods. He reminds me that I am a longtime booster of Tetlock's research, and that I wrote a glowing blurb for [his book]." (L64)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 accurate on both points; omits Scott's second, separate defence.
- **Evidence:**
  - The blurb exists: "Tetlock is one of the very, very best minds in the social sciences today… radical implications for politics, policy, journalism, education, and even epistemology" — Steven Pinker ([Edge, 2015](https://www.edge.org/node/26348#:~:text=Tetlock%20is%20one%20of,social%20sciences%20today)) [V]. Penguin Random House's book page carries a shortened version of it ([PRH](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/227815/superforecasting-by-philip-e-tetlock-and-dan-gardner/); my fetch found "The casual style of Superforecasting belies the profundity of its message" after it) [V; passage not fragment-linkable]. Scott quotes the Edge text word for word.
  - Scott's text: "[Obviously I wouldn't be quoting this paragraph if you weren't the author.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Obviously%20I%20wouldn%E2%80%99t%20be,weren%E2%80%99t%20the%20author.)" [V]. "Longtime booster": Scott does not use the phrase; it is Pinker's gloss of "devoted a quarter of a chapter of your book to why people do this". The earlier report (§1e row 23) found the book gives superforecasting about four pages.
  - Scott's argument is not only "it's Tetlock". He gives [a second defence](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=I%20offered%20an%20additional%20defense%20of%20these%20numbers) (non-frequentist probabilities; the volcano geologist: "saying 'I think there's something like a 5% chance this will erupt in the next week' gives you all the information you need") and cites Tetlock et al. on 25-year forecasts [V]. Pinker's reply L64–L80 answers the Tetlock link at length but never mentions the volcano/communication argument.
  - Scott also says: "I don't claim that there is no conceivable argument for why these probabilistic estimates might be less valuable… I claim that, if you had such an argument, you should have made it" [V]. Pinker's §IV now makes that argument (no deadline, no base rate, not conditioned). That is an answer to Scott, and the reply does not say so.
- **How checked:** fetched Edge page and PRH page; read post.md item 9.

### B4. Chernobyl "dream sequence"
- **Pinker:** "Alexander draws a tenuous analogy to the Chernobyl nuclear disaster, based on a kind of dream sequence in which he imagines what I might have said as a 'techno-optimist' had I been present." (L105)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 the scene is accurately described; the quoted word is not in it, and the point of the passage is misdescribed.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scott: "[Remember the episode in *Chernobyl* where the technicians were arguing about whether a meltdown was imminent? I imagine you walking into the television screen and interjecting yourself into the conversation](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Remember%20the%20episode%20in%20Chernobyl%20where%20the%20technicians%20were%20arguing)": "Fears of nuclear energy are often fanned by bad-faith Luddites… Do you really want to live in a world where our young people hear a constant drumbeat of panic…" [V]. "Dream sequence" fits.
  - The word "techno-optimist" is not in the Chernobyl passage. In the letter it appears in the global-warming paragraph and the closing ("the brand of techno-optimism you promote"). Pinker puts it in quote marks as if it were Scott's label for the imagined speech [V; text search].
  - The point: "[Your speech would be beautiful and philosophically impeccable - but, as a natural fact about the world, the reactor was, in fact, going to melt down. Replacing a physical analysis of the reactor's parameters with a moral analysis of whether technology is good in general does nobody any favors.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Your%20speech%20would%20be%20beautiful%20and%20philosophically%20impeccable)" [V]. It is an analogy about *form of argument* (progress-is-good does not settle whether this reactor is safe), introduced as the reply to Pinker's stated motive from his letter.
  - Pinker's rebuttal ("the analogy in fact goes the other way": what would have prevented Chernobyl was normie safety) is a point about the causes of the accident; Scott's analogy never says what caused it. Whether Pinker's causal account is right is batch E (E4).
- **How checked:** read the passage in both versions; text-searched for "techno-optimist".

In his telling, "1990s techno-optimists, worried that an over-reaction to global warming would threaten economic growth, decided to simply deny its existence. It's all a Chinese hoax, temperature is the same as always, 'hide the decline.'" … "because everyone remembered they'd spent the past thirty years lying through their teeth." (L109)
- **Verdict:** ✅ verbatim in both versions; 🟡 for two cuts.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scott: "[And he would remember the cautionary tale of global warming.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=And%20he%20would%20remember%20the%20cautionary%20tale%20of%20global%20warming.) How 1990s techno-optimists, worried that an over-reaction to global warming would threaten economic growth, decided to simply deny its existence. It's all a Chinese hoax, temperature is the same as always, "hide the decline."" [V]. Same in the live page [V].

Pinker's gloss ("The moral: a sceptic of AI doom is today's climate denier") is stronger than Scott's: the stated worry is that "all of this prevaricating will come back to roost", with "prevaricating" about AI risk generally, and that Pinker "will have discredited yourself" and "poisoned the brand of techno-optimism".

### B6. "dismissed my concerns" about existential dread in young people
- **Pinker:** "Though Alexander dismissed my concerns about sowing existential dread of AI extinction in young people, in 2021 he himself expressed exactly such concern about existential dread of climate extinction." (L115)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Scott did address it, at length; "dismissed" is Pinker's word for a rebuttal.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scott quotes Pinker ("Telling young people they will soon perish en masse has costs in their appreciation of the institutions of modernity, their mental health…") and answers three ways ([item 7](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=I%20think%20that%20insofar,modern%20institutions%20recently)): (1) panic is rarer than elites imagine (Tanner Greer); (2) it proves too much (stock bubble, asteroid); (3) "insofar as young people have lost confidence in modern institutions recently, it's been [partly, after Oct 8] because of a priestly class of academics"; plus the "decreasing fertility → extinction" point ("definitely false" after Oct 8, "definitely obviously false" before) [V].
  - Scott does not argue that dread cannot affect young people; he argues Pinker's claim is a general argument against ever discussing a bad possibility.
- **Context / what's left out:** the reply does not answer Scott's rebuttals on panic or fertility. It answers with a different move (Scott's own 2021 climate essay). Whether that essay says "exactly such concern" is A13.
- **How checked:** read item 7 both versions.

### B7. "GPT-6 is more intelligent than GPT-3" and the g quotes
- **Pinker:** "'GPT-6 is more intelligent than GPT-3,' he writes. 'In the same way, GPT-9 will be more intelligent than GPT-6. At some point, this intelligence will exceed human intelligence. My argument requires nothing more complicated or philosophically dubious than this.' He adds that psychologists have found a general factor of intelligence not just in humans but in rats, primates, and birds; that a single factor explains about 80 percent of the variance in AI benchmark scores; and that 'the most parsimonious explanation is…'" (L131)
- **Verdict:** ✅ verbatim; 🟡 for dropped qualifiers that bear on "doubles down on the one-dimension theory of intelligence".
- **Evidence:**
  - [GPT-6 is more intelligent than GPT-3](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=GPT%2D6%20is%20more%20intelligent%20than%20GPT%2D3). You can split hairs… but common-sensically it's true. In the same way, GPT-9 will be more intelligent than GPT-6. At some point, this intelligence will exceed human intelligence. [My argument requires nothing more complicated or philosophically dubious than this.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=My%20argument%20requires%20nothing,dubious%20than%20this.) [V]. Pinker drops "You can split hairs, you can come up with some obscure sense in which that word is slightly off".
  - Scott: "[For me, the most parsimonious explanation is that AI intelligence is a function of scaling laws](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=For%20me%2C%20the%20most,of%20scaling%20laws) (parameter count and training data) in a way that's pretty close to how animal intelligence is a function of neuron number." Pinker drops "For me", which marks it as Scott's own view. Then Scott: "[But I don't need you to accept my speculative philosophical research program](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=But%20I%20don%E2%80%99t%20need,philosophical%20research%20program) - only the drop-dead obvious fact that GPT-6 is better than GPT-2 in every way" [V]. The reply's "doubles down on the one-dimension theory" and its claim that Scott's case rests on neuron-number extrapolation skip this.
  - Rats: Scott says psychologists "have hardly failed to give IQ tests to rats. They've found have a *g*-like structure" and cites Reader/Hager/Laland (primates) and MacLean et al (birds) [V]. Pinker's "rats, primates, and birds" is a fair compression.
  - 80%: "[Ruan, Maddison, and Hashimoto] find that a single factor (general intelligence) explains about 80% of the variance in an AI's scores on any benchmark" [V]. Pinker's "about 80 percent of the variance in AI benchmark scores" is accurate to the sentence. Whether the paper supports it is G1.
- **Context / what's left out:** Scott concedes the "mystical fluid" worry in his own text: "[the word "smart" is not a perfect pure mathematical identity claiming utter superiority at everything; I might be a better poet than Einstein, and the ant might be better at certain underground navigation tasks than the mouse](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=In%20the%20same%20sense%2C%20Albert)" [V]. Pinker's reply to the ant/mouse point (L137 "ants that navigate their way home") is something Scott had already allowed.
- **How checked:** text search and read.

### B8. "Alexander's parody of sceptics who deny that machines can be 'bigger'"
- **Pinker:** "Alexander's parody of sceptics who deny that machines can be 'bigger' than people misses this. A cruise ship is certainly bigger than a person…" (L135)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Scott did not write the parody; he cites a published joke paper and uses it.
- **Evidence:**
  - "[In 2017, Garfinkel et al published *On The Impossibility Of Super-Sized Machines*](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=In%202017%2C%20Garfinkel%20et%20al%20published), which argues against claims that machines may one day become larger than humans… This paper was, of course, a joke, making fun of the exact argument you are using here. A cruise ship is bigger than a human, no qualifications necessary." ([ACX](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=This%20paper%20was%2C%20of%20course%2C%20a%20joke)) [V]
  - "Parody" is Pinker's word; Scott's is "joke". The cruise-ship sentence is Scott's; the paper and the size/skinny/tall examples are Garfinkel et al.'s (arXiv 1703.10987, per the link in the letter) [2nd, link only; I did not open the paper].
  - Pinker's counter ("knowing that tells you nothing about whether it can climb stairs") answers the point Scott makes (size is an ambiguous-at-the-margins word that is clear at the tails). It does not mention Scott's "ambijectivity" argument.
- **How checked:** read passage; did not open the arXiv paper.

### B9. Sorites: "Age is meaningless", "Lakes don't exist"
- **Pinker:** "Alexander says that my claim that 'superintelligence' is a meaningless concept commits the sorites fallacy… By my logic, he writes, 'Age is meaningless' and 'Lakes don't exist.'" (L157)
- **Verdict:** ✅ on the quotes; 🟡 on "says… commits the sorites fallacy" (that is Pinker's paraphrase, and Scott's sentence is about other people).
- **Evidence:**
  - Scott's bullets: "[Age is meaningless. After all, there's no point at which someone was young yesterday, but old today.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Age%20is%20meaningless.%20After%20all)", "Height is meaningless…", "Lakes don't exist. After all, a puddle is not a lake…" [V]. They are Scott's reductio of Pinker's argument "this could disprove any concept", not statements by Pinker. Pinker's "By my logic, he writes" is a correct framing.
  - The word "sorites" in the letter: "[Several people with philosophical backgrounds commented that this was the *sorites* argument, a well-known pseudo-paradox which shouldn't be employed for real work.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Several%20people%20with%20philosophical%20backgrounds%20commented%20that%20this%20was%20the)" [V]. The phrase "commits the sorites fallacy" is not in the letter.
- **Context / what's left out:** Pinker drops the middle bullet ("Height is meaningless") and Scott's heading ("You employ weird pseudo-arguments that could prove anything", "weird" removed Oct 8). Pinker's rebuttal (the doomers treat superintelligence as an event) is an answer; the earlier report (row 6) rated Scott's criticism of the "meaningless" tweet ✅.
- **How checked:** text search; read item 5.

### B10. Website example of instrumental convergence
- **Pinker:** "Alexander's version: an AI asked to design a website 'can't design the website if it ceases to exist. So now the AI has two goals. … The AI can't design the website or preserve itself if some more powerful person tries to prevent it. So now the AI has three goals: design the website, preserve itself, and become powerful enough to fight off challenges.'" (L199)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 verbatim; the quote is the first of Scott's three reasons, and the reply then rebuts only that one.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scott: "[The AI can't design the website if it ceases to exist.](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=The%20AI%20can%E2%80%99t%20design,ceases%20to%20exist.) So now the AI has two goals: design the website, and preserve its own existence." [V]. Pinker's ellipsis covers "design the website, and preserve its own existence."
  - Scott's own hedges: instrumental goals "[might be less relevant to modern deep-learning based AIs than the other two reasons](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=They%20might%20be%20less,other%20two%20reasons), but could reappear as we get smarter and more coherent agents"; the setup is "suppose this is implemented as a genuine, philosophically-meaningful *goal* rather than simply a set of if-then commands"; and "[Misgeneralization is when humans reinforce certain behaviors in an AI](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=Misgeneralization%20is%20when%20humans%20reinforce%20certain%20behaviors%20in%20an%20AI)… *Reward-hacking*…", with the Hugging Face incident as the example [V].
  - The reply never mentions misgeneralization or reward hacking (text search of the whole reply: no hit for either) [V]. It says "instrumental convergence applies to a hypothetical agent that relentlessly maximises a single objective with no other goals or constraints" (L201), which is Scott's argument 3 (single goal), not his reply to it (see B23).
- **How checked:** text search of reply and letter.
