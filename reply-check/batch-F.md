### F1. Who denied climate science in the 1990s (Scott's version vs Pinker's)
- **Pinker:** "But this history is completely wrong. The campaign to cast doubt on climate science was run not by techno-optimists but by a circle of Cold War physicists and a network of right-wing think tanks intent on preventing regulation of private enterprise. The 'Chinese hoax' line came from Donald Trump, not Silicon Valley, and 'hide the decline' was a phrase that deniers lifted from hacked emails of climate scientists." (L111)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Each of his three facts is right, and Scott's two phrases are anachronistic for "the 1990s". But "completely wrong" is overstated: the same sources say the campaign's motive was fear that over-reaction would hurt markets, which is Scott's motive; and libertarian-leaning optimists (Cato, Julian Simon, later Ridley, Lomborg) did downplay it.
- **Evidence for Pinker:**
  - Scott's text as published: "How 1990s techno-optimists, worried that an over-reaction to global warming would threaten economic growth, decided to simply deny its existence. It's all a Chinese hoax, temperature is the same as always, 'hide the decline'." ([ACX letter](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=How%201990s%20techno%2Doptimists%2C%20worried,deny%20its%20existence.)) [V; also in `../post.md` L392; the live page still has "hide the decline"]
  - Merchants of Doubt (Oreskes & Conway) is about physicists Seitz, Singer, Nierenberg, Jastrow and the Marshall Institute: "joined forces with conservative think tanks and private corporations" ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchants_of_Doubt#:~:text=joined%20forces%20with%20conservative%20think%20tanks%20and%20private%20corporations)) [2nd; Bloomsbury page is Cloudflare-blocked: [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20260917053605/https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/merchants-of-doubt-9781608193943/#:~:text=Merchants%20of%20Doubt)]
  - Trump tweet, 2012-11-06: "The concept of global warming was created by and for the Chinese in order to make U.S. manufacturing non-competitive." (status [265895292191248385](https://twitter.com/realDonaldTrump/status/265895292191248385)) [2nd: date and text from PolitiFact/The Hill snippets, not fetched]. It is 2012, not the 1990s.
  - "Hide the decline": Phil Jones email about a 1999 WMO graph, hacked Nov 2009; "the decline" is the tree-ring divergence problem, "taken out of context by commentators promoting climate change denial" ([Wikipedia, CRU email controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatic_Research_Unit_email_controversy)) [2nd]. So it is 2009, and the phrase is a scientist's, which Pinker says correctly. Scott's "1990s ... hide the decline" is a time-compression.
- **Evidence for Scott (techno-optimist / free-market side minimised or denied):**
  - The same book, per Wikipedia: "They feared that an over-reaction to environmental problems would lead to heavy-handed government intervention in the marketplace" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchants_of_Doubt#:~:text=They%20feared%20that%20an,in%20the%20marketplace)) [2nd]. That is Scott's stated motive almost word for word.
  - "in a 1998 speech, a staff member of the Cato Institute, a libertarian think tank, argued that emission controls' negative economic effects outweighed their environmental benefits" ([Wikipedia, Climate change denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial#:~:text=in%20a%201998%20speech%2C,that%20emission%20controls)) [2nd]. Cato's own line, then and now, is "real but overstated" (minimising, not denying) ([Wikipedia, Cato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute)) [2nd].
  - Julian Simon, the optimist icon: "skeptical, in 1994, of claims that human activity caused global environmental damage, notably in relation to CFCs, ozone depletion and climate change" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Lincoln_Simon#:~:text=Simon%20was%20skeptical%2C%20in,global%20environmental%20damage)) [2nd].
  - Matt Ridley (optimist author, a Pinker friend), WSJ 2014 "Whatever Happened to Global Warming?" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Ridley#:~:text=Whatever%20Happened%20to%20Global%20Warming%3F)) [2nd]; Lomborg called "the pre-eminent luke-warmist" in a source quoted in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial) [2nd]. Both are 2000s-2010s, not 1990s.
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - What I could not find: a documented 1990s *Silicon Valley / tech* optimist camp that denied warming. The documented actors are the physicists, think tanks and Exxon-funded groups, some of them libertarian. So "techno-optimists" is Scott's label for a mostly different group, and "1990s" is partly wrong (Trump 2012, Climategate 2009).
  - Scott's actual point (credibility lost by past denial) does not need the label to be exact; Pinker's rebuttal mostly answers the label.
- **How checked:** fetched Scott's letter and the Wikipedia pages (V); Merchants of Doubt via Wikipedia; Trump tweet from search snippets only.

### F3. Extinction Rebellion, Hallam, Thunberg, tactics, results
- **Pinker:** XR "was launched in 2018 with the warning that 'Scientists, economists and many other credible voices anticipate societal collapse and mass death as inevitable, with human extinction a real possibility,' and with a demand for net-zero emissions by 2025. ... Roger Hallam, told the BBC, 'I am talking about the slaughter, death, and starvation of 6 billion people this century—that's what the science predicts.' Greta Thunberg told the elites at Davos, 'I don't want you to be hopeful. I want you to panic.' The movement's tactics—blocking traffic, gluing hands to roads, throwing soup on paintings—won headlines but otherwise accomplished nothing before it turned its attention to Palestine." (L113)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Quotes are accurate. The XR quote is cut before its condition. Thunberg is not part of XR. Soup was Just Stop Oil. "Accomplished nothing" is contestable, and the Palestine turn is partly supported.
- **Evidence:**
  - XR press text, 2018-10-26: "Scientists, economists and many other credible voices anticipate societal collapse and mass death as inevitable, with human extinction a real possibility, if rapid action is not taken now." ([XR UK](https://extinctionrebellion.uk/2018/10/26/rebellion-against-uk-governments-criminal-inaction-on-climate-breakdown/#:~:text=with%20human%20extinction%20a,not%20taken%20now)) [V]. Pinker's quote stops before "if rapid action is not taken now" (a conditional, like the AI doomers' "if we build it").
  - Demand: "enact legally binding policy measures to reduce carbon emissions to net zero by 2025" ([same page](https://extinctionrebellion.uk/2018/10/26/rebellion-against-uk-governments-criminal-inaction-on-climate-breakdown/#:~:text=enact%20legally%20binding%20policy,zero%20by%202025)) [V]. The same page lists Thunberg as a speaker at the 31 Oct event, and George Monbiot.
  - Hallam: Science Feedback reviews "slaughter, death, and starvation of 6 billion people" (BBC, Aug 2019) and finds it unsupported by published studies; Caldeira: "no peer-reviewed analysis" supports it ([Science Feedback](https://science.feedback.org/review/prediction-extinction-rebellion-climate-change-will-kill-6-billion-people-unsupported-roger-hallam-bbc/#:~:text=slaughter%2C%20death%2C%20and%20starvation%20of%206%20billion%20people)) [V for the quote as printed there].   - Thunberg, Davos 2019: "But I don't want your hope. I don't want you to be hopeful. I want you to panic. I want you to feel the fear I feel every day. And then I want you to act." ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2019/jan/25/our-house-is-on-fire-greta-thunberg16-urges-leaders-to-act-on-climate#:~:text=I%20don%E2%80%99t%20want%20you%20to%20be%20hopeful.%20I%20want%20you%20to%20panic.)) [V, via a Wayback copy]. It is a call to act, with no extinction claim; she was a school striker who spoke at XR events, not an XR officer.
  - Tactics: tomato soup on the Van Gogh was Just Stop Oil, 14 Oct 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Stop_Oil#:~:text=threw%20tomato%20soup%20at,Vincent%20van%20Gogh)) [2nd]; Insulate Britain and JSO are XR "offshoots" formed in 2021 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction_Rebellion#:~:text=Offshoots%3A%20Insulate%20Britain%20and%20Just%20Stop%20Oil)) [2nd].
  - Palestine: JSO's coordinating "Umbrella" includes Youth Demand ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Stop_Oil#:~:text=Youth%20Demand)) [2nd], which is a pro-Palestine climate group (the Standard, 2025, cited there).   - Results (for "accomplished nothing"): search snippets say Parliament declared a climate emergency (1 May 2019, motion tabled by Corbyn) after XR's April 2019 protests, and the UK legislated net zero by 2050 in June 2019 (XR called it too little) ([Al Jazeera, 2019-07-15](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2019/7/15/extinction-rebellion-climate-protests-spread-across-uk)) [2nd, not fetched]. ### F4. What actually reduced emissions
- **Pinker:** "what has actually reduced emissions in developed countries has come from normie policy and engineering: cheap solar and wind, batteries, the switch from coal to gas, and carbon pricing where it has been tried." (L113)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 Broadly supported, but incomplete: lower energy use (partly slower GDP growth) and efficiency policy are on the list in the best decomposition study, and "cheap solar and wind" came out of subsidy policy; batteries and carbon pricing are not what that study found.
- **Evidence:**
  - Le Quéré et al., Nature Climate Change 2019 (18 developed economies, 2005-2015): "the displacement of fossil fuels by renewable energy and decreases in energy use explain decreasing CO2 emissions. However, the decrease in energy use can be explained at least in part by a lower growth in gross domestic product." ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-019-0419-7#:~:text=the%20displacement%20of%20fossil,explain%20decreasing%20CO)) [V]. "Policies on renewable energy are supporting emissions reductions ... and policies on energy efficiency are supporting lower energy use" (same abstract, correlational) [V].
  - A search snippet from the same paper's full text: in the US the biggest driver was coal-to-gas switching (fracking) [2nd]. IEA CO2 Emissions in 2023: in advanced economies "clean energy growth" was half of the 2023 fall, and in the US coal-to-gas was the top driver [2nd, snippet].
  - Carbon Brief on the UK's 2023 fall (to the lowest since 1879): two-thirds from lower gas demand, coal down 23%; "mostly unrelated to deliberate climate action" (weather, imports, high prices) [2nd, snippet]. So a single-year drop is not good evidence for any of Pinker's four causes.
  - Counter-view on the US, 1997-2013: after 2007 the decline came mostly from the recession (Feng et al., PMC4518269) [2nd, snippet].
- **Context / what's left out:** I found nothing in this pass on carbon pricing's measured effect (EU ETS, UK carbon price floor); Pinker's "where it has been tried" is hedged and I did not test it. His list is the reasonable consensus list, minus efficiency, demand and offshoring.
- **How checked:** fetched the Nature abstract (V); the rest from search snippets.

### F6. Interwar gas bombing; Einstein 1950; "would not make it to 2000"
- **Pinker:** "After World War I, military theorists and politicians predicted that humanity would be extinguished by fleets of planes blanketing cities with poison gas like crop-dusters. In 1950, Albert Einstein not-so-presciently proposed, 'Only the creation of a world government can prevent the impending self-destruction of mankind.' The prophecy would be echoed by decades of scientists and international-relations experts who told my generation that we would not make it to the year 2000." (L235)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 The Einstein sentence is real and dated 1950 by a secondary source, but it is a conditional policy claim, not a forecast; "extinguished" is stronger than what I found for the interwar fear; the "year 2000" claim is plausible but unsourced.
- **Evidence:**
  - Einstein: John Mueller, writing on the early Cold War, "he wrote in 1950, 'Unless we are able, in the near future, to abolish the mutual fear of military aggression, we are doomed.' ... 'Only the creation of a world government can prevent the impending self-destruction of mankind.'" ([Mueller, doom.pdf, PDF p. 110](https://politicalscience.osu.edu/faculty/jmueller/doom.pdf#page=110)) [V]. His note 9 cites Einstein 1960 (*Einstein on Peace*, ed. Nathan & Norden), pp. 347, 533, 566. I could not reach the primary document, so the exact 1950 occasion is [2nd].
  - Reading: "Only X can prevent Y" is a claim that world government is necessary, made to promote it. The Mueller passage is about *proposals* to avert doom, so "prophecy" fits only loosely, and Pinker himself says "proposed".
  - Interwar: Baldwin, 10 Nov 1932: "the bomber will always get through", and the "only defence is in offence, which means that you have to kill more women and children more quickly than the enemy" ([Baldwin speech, CSIS PDF](https://missilethreat.csis.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/A-Fear-for-the-Future.pdf)) [2nd, snippet]. Liddell Hart guessed 250,000 casualties in the first week; the Bloomsbury study *The Coming of the Aerial War* describes mass fear of gas and bombing. [2nd] None of the snippets say "humanity extinguished"; "end of civilisation" is the typical wording. I did not find the extinction version.
  - "Would not make it to 2000": polls in the 1980s found, e.g., 44% of University of New Hampshire students judging nuclear war before 2000 at least 50% likely and 88% not expecting to survive (Rendall 2022, *Global Policy*) [2nd, snippet]; a 1985 Gallup found 48% of British teens thought nuclear war likely [2nd]. That supports the belief among the public, less so "scientists and international-relations experts" saying it would happen. Experts did warn (Doomsday Clock, nuclear winter); I did not source them.
- **How checked:** fetched Mueller's PDF (V); the rest from search snippets.

### F7. Ehrlich, Club of Rome, Bill Joy, Y2K, "hair on fire"
- **Pinker:** "the biologist Paul Ehrlich's *The Population Bomb* (1968), which announced that 'The battle to feed all of humanity is over'; the Club of Rome's 1972 computer simulation showing how resource shortages would lead to a population crash and collapse of industrial civilisation; the 2000 warning by the technologist Bill Joy that nanotech would 'make humans an endangered species' by engulfing them with self-replicating 'gray goo'; the Y2K bug that would black out cities, crash planes, and launch ICBMs; and the post-9/11 warning that 'our hair is on fire' because terrorists had the means to detonate nuclear bombs hidden in suitcases and shipping containers." (L237)
- **Verdict:** Ehrlich ✅. Club of Rome 🟡. Bill Joy 🟡. Y2K 🟡. "Hair on fire" 🟡 (post-9/11 is right; the details are unverified). Overall 🟡: each is a real example; each leaves out a qualification.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Ehrlich.** Prologue of the revised (1971) edition: "The battle to feed all of humanity is over. In the 1970s and 1980s hundreds of millions of people will starve to death in spite of any crash programs embarked upon now." ([LSU scan, PDF p. 2](https://biotech.law.lsu.edu/blog/Ehrlich-Population-Bomb-forward.pdf#page=2)) [V]. The 1968 first edition has "In the 1970's the world will undergo famines—hundreds of millions of people are going to starve to death" (Reason, Smithsonian) [2nd]. Pinker's quote is verbatim in both.
  - **Limits to Growth.** "If the present growth trends ... continue unchanged, the limits to growth on this planet will be reached sometime within the next one hundred years. The most probable result will be a rather sudden and uncontrollable decline in both population and industrial capacity." ([1972 text, PDF p. 25](http://www.donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/Limits-to-Growth-digital-scan-version.pdf#page=25)) [V]. Standard run: "clearly that of overshoot and collapse" from nonrenewable resource depletion ([PDF p. 126](http://www.donellameadows.org/wp-content/userfiles/Limits-to-Growth-digital-scan-version.pdf#page=126)) [V]. Against Pinker's use: the book says its graphs "are not exact predictions of the values of the variables at any particular year in the future. They are indications of the system's behavioral tendencies only" (printed text near the "prediction" discussion; pdftotext line 3994 of my copy) [V], its conclusion 2 is that growth trends can be altered to a sustainable equilibrium, and the run's collapse is a 21st-century event, so it has not "failed" by date. The Scoreboard (F5) lists it as Active, with 2021 and 2024 updates saying trends track the business-as-usual run [2nd, via JSON].
  - **Bill Joy.** Wired's subhead: "Our most powerful 21st-century technologies—robotics, genetic engineering, and nanotech—are threatening to make humans an endangered species." ([Wired, 2000-04-01](https://www.wired.com/2000/04/joy-2/#:~:text=are%20threatening%20to%20make%20humans%20an%20endangered%20species)) [V]. So the phrase is about three technologies, not nanotech. Gray goo: "Among the cognoscenti of nanotechnology, this threat has become known as the 'gray goo problem'" ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/2000/04/joy-2/#:~:text=this%20threat%20has%20become%20known%20as%20the%20%E2%80%9Cgray%20goo%20problem.%E2%80%9D)) [V], and replicators "could easily be too tough, small, and rapidly spreading to stop—at least if we make no preparation" [V]. Joy frames it conditionally: "if our own extinction is a likely, or even possible, outcome of our technological development, shouldn't we proceed with great caution?" ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/2000/04/joy-2/#:~:text=if%20our%20own%20extinction,with%20great%20caution)) [V], and his remedy is "relinquishment" [V]. "Would" overstates his hedged claim.
  - **Y2K.** Total remediation cost "likely surpassed US$300 billion" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2000_problem#:~:text=The%20total%20cost%20of,surpassed%20US%24300%20billion)) [2nd]. The US and Russia set up a "Center for Year 2000 Strategic Stability ... to mitigate the possibility of false positive readings in each nation's nuclear attack early warning systems" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2000_problem#:~:text=Center%20for%20Year%202000%20Strategic%20Stability)) [2nd]. So governments took a missile-warning failure seriously, and "launch ICBMs" is a popular version of that concern. The usual defence is that few failures occurred because the work was done; Pinker offers it as a false alarm and the record is disputed. Direct "launch ICBMs" predictions: I did not find an attributable source.
  - **"Hair on fire."** The phrase is the title of a Wall Street Journal op-ed, "'Our Hair Is on Fire'", by Rudman, Hart, Gelb and Flynn, 16 Dec 2004, which "declar[ed] that al-Qaeda had both the ability and the intent to detonate a weapon of mass destruction in the United States and envision[ed] graves by the hundreds of thousands" ([Mueller & Stewart, HSAJ](https://www.hsaj.org/articles/43#:~:text=%E2%80%9C%E2%80%99Our%20Hair%20Is%20on,and%20the%20intent)) [V, secondary description of the op-ed]. That is post-9/11. A different, earlier use: the 9/11 Commission asked Richard Clarke, 24 Mar 2004: "the phrase which has received some currency in our hearings of someone's hair being on fire originated with you" ([hearing](https://9-11commission.gov/archive/hearing8/9-11Commission_Hearing_2004-03-24.htm#:~:text=the%20phrase%20which%20has,originated%20with%20you)) [V]; that is about the summer-2001 pre-9/11 threat reports. Pinker's "post-9/11" is right for the op-ed.
- **Context / what's left out:** I did not read the 2004 op-ed itself (WSJ paywall), so "nuclear bombs hidden in suitcases and shipping containers" is unverified. Mueller's description says "a weapon of mass destruction". I could not check EN ("See EN for sources") because Google Books was blocked.
- **How checked:** fetched the Ehrlich scan, LtG PDF, Wired, Mueller & Stewart, 9/11 hearing (V); Y2K and others from Wikipedia and snippets.

### F8. Hinton and radiologists; mass unemployment; NBER w34243

### F9. Tetlock's expert political judgment
- **Pinker:** "Tetlock's own research on expert political judgment explains why experts are so prone to such errors: they overgeneralise the deference they enjoy in their narrow specialty to the much messier world of politics, history, economics, and human behaviour." (L239)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 (leaning ❌ on the mechanism). Tetlock found experts overconfident and barely better than chance; he did not find that they generalise specialty-deference outward. His data show the largest hedgehog penalty *inside* experts' own domains.
- **Evidence:**
  - Tetlock, in his own words (80,000 Hours post): "experts thought they knew more about the future than they did. ... When experts judged events to be 100 percent slam-dunks, those events occurred, roughly, 80 percent of the time ... they failed to beat: (a) sophisticated dilettantes (experts making predictions outside their specialty ...); (b) extrapolation algorithms" ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/prof-tetlock-predicting-the-future/#:~:text=And%20they%20failed%20to%20beat%3A%20%28a%29%20sophisticated%20dilettantes)) [V]. So experts outside their specialty did about as well as inside it.
  - "The experts surest of their big-picture grasp ... the Isaiah Berlin-style 'hedgehogs', performed worse than their more diffident colleagues, or 'foxes' ... That differential was particularly pronounced for long-range forecasts inside experts' domains of expertise." ([80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/prof-tetlock-predicting-the-future/#:~:text=That%20differential%20was%20particularly,domains%20of%20expertise.)) [V]
  - "Most important, beware of sweeping generalizations. Hedgehogs were not always the worst forecasters." ([same](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/prof-tetlock-predicting-the-future/#:~:text=Most%20important%2C%20beware%20of%20sweeping%20generalizations.)) [V]
