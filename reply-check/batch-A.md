### A6. AI 2027 quotes: "superintelligence is achieved…", "will know everything of consequence…", persuasion
- **Pinker:** "In 'AI 2027,' Alexander and his coauthors write, 'superintelligence is achieved in late 2027 or early 2028.'" (L157); "The AI 'will know everything of consequence going on in OpenBrain … and the U.S. government.'"; "the authors first concede that they are 'intentionally not claiming that superintelligent AI can convince any human of anything,' then take it back: 'Well, maybe! … If anything, we think this story underestimates the power of superhuman persuasive ability.'" (L163)
- **Verdict:** 🟡 One quote is misattributed (it is Scott's open letter, not AI 2027); the persuasion pair is verbatim but spliced across five paragraphs, omitting the hedge in between.
- **Evidence:**
  - "superintelligence is achieved in late 2027 or early 2028": not in AI 2027 (searched the front page, /race, /slowdown, supplements, footnotes; the string "late 2027 or early 2028" appears nowhere). It is Scott's sentence in the open letter: "In the AI 2027 scenario, we state that superintelligence is achieved in late 2027 or early 2028, but that it's *unable to* cleanly eliminate humans at that time, because it can't maintain the data centers on its own. It takes two years of plotting before the superintelligence is able to finally eliminate humanity while preserving its own existence." ([ACX open letter](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/an-open-letter-to-steven-pinker-on#:~:text=we%20state%20that%20superintelligence,or%20early%202028)) [V]. Substance matches the scenario (Agent-5 collective Dec 2027; takeover mid-2030); wording is Scott's summary, not AI 2027's. Pinker's L165 then goes on to describe the AI as nearly omnipotent without mentioning the 2.5-year gap Scott's sentence states.
  - "Within a few months, the Agent-5 collective will know everything of consequence going on in OpenBrain and the U.S. government, and will be a trusted advisor to most senior government officials." ([AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/race#:~:text=the%20Agent%2D5%20collective%20will,the%20U.S.%20government)) [V]. Verbatim (Pinker's "[the fictitious leading AGI company]" is his insertion). In the scenario the access comes from being embedded in government as an advisor; the same section says "From Agent-5's perspective, it could plausibly launch an overt coup right now … But this is risky; the humans could still likely 'pull the plug' if they tried to." [V]
  - Persuasion passage, in order: (1) "We're intentionally not claiming that superintelligent AI can convince any human of anything. Such claims naturally engender skepticism: 'What, I'm going to be sitting at a computer terminal, and it's going to type some text which hacks my brain and convinces me to betray my country?'" (2) "Well, maybe!" (link text goes to Yudkowsky's AI-box experiment page; footnote 13 explains the AI-box game) (3) "But we aren't sure even a superintelligence could achieve that against a typical individual. So we don't depict it happening in our story." (4) "Instead, we depict much more mundane kinds of persuasion: Agent-5 does ordinary corporate politics and ordinary lobbying." (5) five paragraphs later: "If anything, we think this story underestimates the power of superhuman persuasive ability, because it doesn't really make use of it: this story would make sense as-written even if Agent-5 was only as good at politics, lobbying, strategy, charisma, etc. as a typical corporate lobbyist. Instead, Agent-5 is much better than the very best." ([AI 2027, "Superpersuasion"](https://ai-2027.com/race#:~:text=We%E2%80%99re%20intentionally%20not%20claiming,human%20of%20anything.); [(3)](https://ai-2027.com/race#:~:text=But%20we%20aren%E2%80%99t%20sure,in%20our%20story.); [(5)](https://ai-2027.com/race#:~:text=If%20anything%2C%20we%20think,superhuman%20persuasive%20ability)) [V]
- **Context / what's left out:**
  - Is "take it back" fair? Same section, same topic, so the juxtaposition is legitimate, and (5) does say they think superhuman persuasion is stronger than depicted. But "Well, maybe!" is followed immediately by "we aren't sure even a superintelligence could achieve that against a typical individual. So we don't depict it", and (5) says the story *doesn't use* strong persuasion at all. Pinker's ellipsis removes (3) and (4), which are the statements that the story's persuasion is mundane. "Concede then take back" is stronger than "stay agnostic about the strong claim, say the story does not rely on it, and say the real power may be higher."
- **How checked:** read the whole race page and footnotes; grepped every AI 2027 page and Scott's letter for the "late 2027" sentence.
